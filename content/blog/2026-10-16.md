## What do you want to measure?

Choose an assay by the claim its result must support. “Is it healthy?”, “Does it work?”, and “Has it changed?” become answerable only after you specify the property, specimen, time interval, and competing explanations. A sensitive measurement of the wrong property still leaves the biological question unanswered.

This guide helps researchers turn biological questions into measurement claims and compare suitable assay families. It covers selected molecular, cellular, tissue, organismal, clinical, product-quality, plant, and environmental applications. The aim is a justified shortlist and confirmation plan; a family name alone does not specify a validated method.

**Use the guide in three passes:** find your question in the table below; compare the linked methods; complete the [selection worksheet](#your-assay-selection-worksheet). For a known method, use the [assay and acronym index](#assay-and-acronym-index). The [worked selections](#three-worked-selections) show how complementary measurements answer different parts of one question.

## Turn the construct into a measurement claim

“Health,” “stress,” “viability,” and “function” describe constructs: biological ideas whose meanings depend on the question. Cell health might mean maintaining membrane integrity, sustaining energy metabolism, continuing DNA synthesis, or retaining a specialized function. Plant stress might mean a photochemical change, lower carbon assimilation, or reduced growth. A proxy useful for one meaning may leave another entirely unmeasured. [34,35,72]

The selection sequence is **research question → biological construct → measurable property and specimen → observable and endpoint → assay family → candidate comparison and confirmation**. Its practical product is a claim sentence: *In this specimen or system, over this biological interval, I need to determine this property, expressed by this endpoint, well enough to distinguish these alternatives.* The sequence is a reasoning framework; it does not replace validation for a particular intended use.

An **analyte** is the component being examined; a **measurand** is the quantity intended to be measured, specified with its system and conditions. “Protein X” names an analyte but leaves open concentration, catalytic activity, binding, modification, and localization. The **observable** is acquired signal: photons, ion current, voltage, images, or recorded events. The **endpoint** is the reported result after classification, calibration, normalization, or fitting. A fluorescence trace, a relative fold change, and a disease interpretation occupy different stages of the chain. [76]

| Kind of claim | What must be specified | A common overreach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Presence | Target identity, specimen, detection capability, and the meaning of a negative | Below detection becomes “biologically absent.” |
| Quantity | Concentration, amount, fraction, or count; calibration and denominator | Relative intensity becomes absolute concentration. |
| Activity | Reaction or process rate in the represented system | Protein or gene abundance becomes realized function. |
| Binding | Interaction, model, affinity/kinetics, and multivalency | Binding becomes efficacy or protection. |
| Location | Coordinates, sampling unit, registration, and attribution | Co-localization becomes direct contact or signaling. |
| Dynamics | Interval, time resolution, and response definition | A static pool becomes a synthesis or turnover rate. |
| Functional consequence | The actual outcome and relevant biological system | A proximal marker becomes successful killing, delivery, or clinical benefit. |

These distinctions recur across the molecular, cellular, spatial, immune, and product-quality evidence. “Direct for a property” still means conditional on the measurement model: calibrated ATP signal can establish something about ATP while remaining a proxy for surviving cells. A counted transcript is a detected and assigned molecule, not proof that every source molecule was recovered. [34,70,77]

Keep four classification dimensions separate. **Principle** is how a property generates signal; **biological scale** is molecular, cellular, tissue, or organismal context; **format** is bulk, single-cell, spatial, multiplexed, or longitudinal organization; **application** is the intended research, clinical, environmental, or quality decision. Mass spectrometry can serve all those applications. A three-dimensional culture is a model, and high-throughput screening is an arrangement of measurements. Neither specifies one measurand. [78,79]

A changed format can improve access to heterogeneity or timing without changing the underlying property. It can also alter sampling, artifacts, calibration, or even the biological system. Droplet and chamber dPCR share occupancy logic but require appropriate volume information; single-cell proteomics is not simply bulk proteomics with guaranteed identical precision; moving from a secretion spot to intracellular staining changes what and when the immune response is observed. Demonstrate comparability at the endpoint level rather than infer it from a family name. [14,48,80]

## Make each shortlist defensible

Compare methods only after fixing the claim. The following criteria apply across the family comparisons; a missing field remains a reason to obtain information, rather than a value to guess.

| Criterion | What to establish for each candidate | Why it can change the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specimen and system | Matrix, preservation state, biological competence, and representative sampling | A method can be analytically sound yet examine the wrong biological state. |
| Endpoint and units | Raw observable, transformation, calibration, denominator, and model | Concentration, relative intensity, ordinal grade, and fitted rate are different results. |
| Sample use | Material demand, destructiveness, and compatibility with follow-up | Lytic ATP measurements consume cells; LDH supernatant sampling can leave a cellular layer. |
| Temporal/spatial information | Effective resolution and attribution, not only acquisition spacing | A spatial grid may cross cells; extracellular network activity differs from channel current. |
| Equipment and analysis | Required detection, calibration, specialist skills, storage, and computation | NMR, high-resolution MS, spatial imaging, and inverse mechanical models impose different requirements. |
| Throughput | The whole specimen-to-result chain and required confirmation | Fast acquisition can coexist with substantial preparation or analysis. |
| Validation context | Intended use, matrix, range, references, and comparator evidence | Research characterization, clinical decisions, and product release need different supporting evidence. |

A meaningful practical comparison uses the same specimen, endpoint, required performance, and accounting boundary. Acquisition time alone is not turnaround time; a quoted sample volume may exclude repeats and confirmation. Record total cost per interpretable result, including preparation, failed samples, repeats, analysis, and confirmation. Universal cost, consumption, or throughput rankings are not justified across unmatched assays.

Controls should answer named doubts. Blanks assess background; positive controls assess system competence; specificity controls examine recognition; calibration references support quantity; recovery and matrix controls examine loss or altered response. Technical repetition estimates repeatability but may preserve the same interference. **Orthogonal confirmation** tests the same claim through a different measurement principle. **Complementary measurements** examine other properties needed for the broader biological conclusion. [34,81]

The **limit of blank**, **limit of detection**, and **limit of quantitation** respectively concern blank response, reliable distinction from background, and acceptable quantitative performance. A detectable result may remain below the quantitative range. A valid negative, analytical failure, missing observation, and censored value are distinct. Precision describes spread; bias describes systematic difference from a relevant reference. High correlation between methods does not establish agreement or interchangeability. [76,82,83]

Calibration itself needs context. Traceability links a result through a calibration chain with uncertainty at each step; a reference material's **commutability** concerns whether it behaves appropriately across methods for the intended specimen comparison. Type A evaluation estimates uncertainty statistically from observations; Type B evaluation uses other information, such as calibration certificates or prior knowledge. The distinction concerns the evaluation method, not whether variation is biological or instrumental. Many cells, fields, sequence reads, or repeated times within one specimen do not automatically provide independent biological replicates. [76,84,85,86]

Represent the final selection in a record that another reader can interpret: specimen, measured property, principle, method/variant, observable, analysis model, endpoint/unit, controls, calibration, and intended claim. OBI and BAO describe processes or screening concepts; LOINC structures clinical observations; ISA connects investigation, specimens, and assays; EDAM describes computational operations. Reporting frameworks help preserve provenance. Their terms should be mapped to the actual measured property and analysis model; a checklist or ontology identifier does not validate a measurement. [78,79,87,88,89]

The comparisons address measurement, selection, and interpretation. Method names identify families or implementations, not universally transferable specifications. Numerical limits, clinical interpretation, and product decisions require evidence for the particular specimen, platform version, and intended use. The [source and scope note](#source-and-scope-note) describes coverage and interpretation boundaries.

### Define performance before collecting the decisive data

Translate “good enough” into acceptance criteria for the intended claim. A presence/absence decision needs a detection rule and a plan for invalid or indeterminate results. A quantitative comparison needs a usable range, acceptable precision and bias, and a denominator. A fitted rate or potency needs a supported model and uncertainty. Specify those requirements before examining the decisive results; sensitivity alone is not a complete performance target. [76,81,82]

Treat specimen handling as part of the measurement. Record collection, transport, preservation, extraction, storage, and the interval to measurement. Match stability and recovery evidence to that chain. Define the independent experimental unit, distinguish biological replication from repeated measurements, and plan how batches, missing observations, and exclusions will be handled. More wells or cells cannot repair a study with only one independent biological unit. [14,81,86,141]

Validation frameworks have different scopes. ICH Q2(R2) addresses analytical-procedure validation, with Q14 addressing development; ICH M10 addresses drug and active-metabolite bioanalysis within its stated scope, rather than all biomarker or immunogenicity assays. Clinical classification and product release add their own intended-use requirements. Select the applicable framework after specifying the decision, and document which method or software changes would require performance to be reassessed. [51,70,81,141,253]

## Binding, catalysis, and molecular structure

Binding strength, binding speed, catalytic inhibition, assembly, and stability are related questions with different answers. The equilibrium dissociation constant, **KD**, has concentration units; association and dissociation rates describe approach to and escape from a complex. An **IC50** is the concentration producing half-inhibition in the particular assay, whereas a **Ki** requires an appropriate inhibition model. **Avidity** includes multivalent retention and rebinding, so a slowly dissociating multivalent interaction is not necessarily strong single-site affinity. [1,7]

### Choose interaction measurements by the parameter needed

SPR and BLI are optical interaction measurements; ITC measures heat. STD means saturation-transfer-difference NMR, while WaterLOGSY is a water–ligand magnetization-transfer method. Their common binding purpose does not make their acquired signals equivalent. [1,2,4,90]

| Candidate and system | Principle → observable → endpoint | What separates it from alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface plasmon resonance, **SPR**, with characterized interaction partners | Binding-associated refractive-index change → sensorgram in response units → fitted kon (M⁻¹ s⁻¹), koff (s⁻¹), KD (M) | Useful for time-resolved interaction kinetics; surface accumulation and transport must fit the model. [1,91] |
| Bio-layer interferometry, **BLI**, with an immobilized partner | Change in optical-layer interference → wavelength shift over time → fitted kinetics and KD | Real-time interaction measurements with a biosensor-bound partner; surface behavior, transport, and the chosen kinetic model remain part of the interpretation. [1] |
| Isothermal titration calorimetry, **ITC**, in solution | Binding-associated heat → thermal power/integrated heat → stoichiometry, KD, enthalpy | Choose when thermodynamics and stoichiometry matter. Dilution and binding-linked proton exchange can contribute to the observed heat. [2,248] |
| Fluorescence anisotropy/polarization of a labeled species | Polarized emission → anisotropy or polarization → a model-dependent binding estimate | Binding can change rotational motion, but viscosity and temperature also affect rotation. State which optical quantity is reported; rotational correlation time requires an appropriate model or time-resolved measurement. [3,249] |
| Ligand-observed **STD-NMR/WaterLOGSY** with purified targets and fragments | Magnetization transfer → one-dimensional proton spectra → difference/amplification or phase/intensity response, sometimes fitted affinity | Useful for weak-fragment interaction evidence and orthogonal confirmation. Check NMR sensitivity, specimen demand, and throughput for the intended implementation. [4,5,90] |

SPR and BLI observe material associated with a sensing layer. Reference and background signals help distinguish specific accumulation from nonspecific binding, bulk optical shifts, and drift. If delivery is slower than intrinsic association, an apparent rate can describe mass transport rather than reaction chemistry. Rebinding, inactive reagent, heterogeneous partners, and multivalency can also invalidate a simple one-to-one fit. A kinetic curve supports interaction under its model; signaling and functional consequences require separate evidence. [1,7,91]

ITC complements optical kinetics by measuring solution thermodynamics. Ligand-into-buffer measurements address dilution heat; binding-linked proton exchange can add a buffer-dependent contribution. Report the fitted model, concentrations, and uncertainty rather than treating every integrated heat change as intrinsic binding enthalpy. [2,248]

Anisotropy reports rotational behavior that can change with binding. Ligand-observed NMR instead detects magnetization-transfer effects. Select the method whose physical contrast and controls fit the interaction, and use complementary evidence when an artifact could explain the response. Neither signal alone supplies a complete three-dimensional binding site. [3,4,5,249]

### Separate catalytic rate from a binding signal

For a catalytic question, first decide whether the required endpoint is an overall reaction rate or an individual mechanistic step. A steady-state activity assay measures substrate consumption or product formation over an interpretable interval. A calibrated rate can be reported as amount or concentration per time; normalization to enzyme quantity adds an explicit denominator. An endpoint amount divided by elapsed time is not automatically an initial rate, and an apparent Michaelis constant is not generally a binding dissociation constant. [6]

| Starting choice | Choose it when | Prefer another route when | Evidence that makes the result interpretable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct activity measurement** | The substrate or product has a distinguishable optical, chemical, or MS signal | The native transformation lacks a selective, accessible readout | Chemical identity, detector response, background, and a valid rate interval |
| **Coupled activity measurement** | A linked reporter converts the target reaction into a measurable signal | The candidate affects the coupling or reporter, or the reporter constrains the observed response | Separation of target-reaction effects from reporter-chain interference |
| **Transient kinetics** | The question concerns early steps or intermediates hidden in the overall rate | The decision needs only a reproducible overall activity comparison | Instrument time resolution and a mechanistic model supported by the trace |

These choices separate the biological reaction from its detection chain. Direct measurement still has analytical artifacts; a coupled signal needs evidence that its change can be attributed to the target reaction. [6,81]

Stopped-flow records early optical reaction traces; quench-flow examines products associated with a defined reaction age. Fitted microscopic rates can help investigate steps hidden by a steady-state aggregate, but instrument dead time can hide the earliest behavior and different mechanisms can fit similar decays. Baselines, mixing artifacts, and the proposed reaction model determine which mechanisms the trace can distinguish. These candidates need rapid-reaction instrumentation plus optical or product-analysis capacity. [92]

Tight-binding inhibition needs a mass-balance treatment when enzyme binding materially depletes free inhibitor. The Morrison model uses measured velocities and active-enzyme information to estimate an apparent inhibition constant in concentration units. Unknown active fraction and incomplete equilibration can confuse tight and slow binding. Ordinary IC50-to-Ki conversion is justified only under its competition and concentration assumptions. For slow-binding inhibition, activation, or biochemical reconstitution, the rate regime, active fraction, and reporter chain must support the proposed model; a familiar equation cannot supply missing mechanistic evidence. [7]

Förster resonance energy-transfer methods, including FRET/TR-FRET, and AlphaScreen supply interaction-associated signals in screening formats. AlphaScreen's proximity-dependent singlet-oxygen luminescence is vulnerable to quenching and chemical interference. Detection-chemistry counter-screens can test whether a compound changes the reporter instead of the biology. Screen-control separation, including a Z-prime statistic, assesses assay robustness rather than target specificity; a structural interference alert is a prediction, not demonstrated interference. [93,94,95]

### Assembly and structure require their own measurements

The assembly shortlist includes sedimentation-velocity analytical ultracentrifugation (SV-AUC), while differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF) addresses unfolding-associated behavior. Hydrogen–deuterium exchange MS (HDX-MS), fast photochemical oxidation of proteins (FPOP), and cross-linking MS (XL-MS) supply different structural constraints. [8,10,11,12]

| Required property | Supported candidates and endpoints | Essential qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solution heterogeneity and assembly | **SV-AUC:** radial/time optical boundaries → sedimentation distributions, coefficients in Svedbergs, and modeled mass estimates | Density, viscosity, and model assumptions matter; rapidly exchanging species can resemble one stable intermediate. [8,96] |
| Individual-event mass distribution | **Mass photometry:** interferometric video contrast → calibrated masses, commonly kDa | Composition-dependent contrast and event selection qualify population inference; protein standards do not calibrate every composition identically. [9,97] |
| Unfolding-associated transitions | **nanoDSF/dye-based DSF:** fluorescence versus temperature → apparent transition or melting temperature (°C) | The fluorescence response must be suitable for the protein and model. Aggregation requires an appropriate additional observable; thermal stability is distinct from activity or cellular engagement. [10,98] |
| Backbone protection and dynamics | **HDX-MS:** isotope mass shifts → deuterium uptake in Da over time | Exchange chemistry and back-exchange qualify structural inference; apparent population patterns can be artifacts. [11] |
| Side-chain exposure | **FPOP:** oxidative labeling → modified/unmodified peptide frequencies | Radical exposure and buffer chemistry must be accounted for; label-induced change can distort native-state interpretation. [99] |
| Compatible residue-pair proximity | **XL-MS:** linked peptide spectra → reagent-dependent distance restraints | Linkage-level false-discovery control and transient collisions matter; restraints do not define a unique structure. [12,100] |
| Reconstructed density and local resolvability | **Cryo-EM/ET**, with directional FSC and Q-score analysis | Acquisition, reconstruction, and validation are separate; preferred orientation or local map quality can limit coordinates. [101] |

SV-AUC estimates hydrodynamic distributions, while mass photometry reports calibrated masses for detected events. Choose between them using the assembly question, concentration range, specimen composition, and recovery of the populations of interest. Their shared label-free format does not establish equivalent population sampling or a universal ranking for material demand and turnaround time. [8,9,96]

HDX, oxidative footprinting, and cross-links provide different structural constraints. Agreement can strengthen a structural interpretation, but exposure and proximity still need to be connected to biological function by a separate measurement. [11,12,99]

## DNA, RNA, and regulatory state

Ask whether you need a specified sequence, its abundance, linkage along a molecule, a cellular distribution, a base modification, or chromatin context. Targeted amplification can answer a narrow quantity question without describing the transcriptome; an accessibility map does not itself establish gene expression. For RNA, steady-state abundance combines synthesis and degradation and does not establish protein translation. [20,21,22,102]

### Targeted presence and quantity

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) tracks amplification; RT-qPCR adds reverse transcription for RNA. Digital PCR (dPCR) uses partition occupancy. Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) offer constant-temperature detection routes. [13,14,15,16]

| Candidate | Observable and reported result | Controls and selection boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **qPCR/RT-qPCR** | Fluorescence versus amplification cycle → Cq/Ct; calibration or efficiency-aware normalization → concentration or relative fold change | No-template, no-RT, reference-gene stability, and efficiency evidence address different errors. [103] Choose targeted expression or quantity; Cq itself is not a transcript count. [13,104] |
| **Digital PCR**, droplet or chamber | Endpoint positive/negative partitions → occupancy model → copies/µL or variant allele fraction | Blank and wild-type-only references, classification, partition volume, and saturation qualify quantity. Choose when the partition-based model fits the claim. [14,105] |
| **LAMP/RPA**, with reverse transcription for RNA targets as appropriate | Constant-temperature amplification → fluorescence, color, or turbidity → detection or time to positivity | Positive and negative controls address assay competence and contamination. Validate specificity and the reported endpoint; quantitative and multiplex claims require evidence for that implementation. [15,16,106,107] |

Conventional relative RT-qPCR analysis assumes an appropriate target/reference efficiency relationship. Efficiency-aware analysis, including the Pfaffl approach, does not solve unstable references, RNA degradation, poor extraction, or unequal reverse transcription. A real-time thermocycler and target-specific validation are required; selecting a faster or simpler detection chemistry cannot replace them. MIQE 2.0 provides an updated reporting framework for judging whether these decisions are adequately documented. [13,104,108]

dPCR can estimate target concentration without a conventional external calibration curve, but uncertainty remains. Positive partitions can contain multiple molecules: the occupancy estimate is λ = −ln(1−p), where p is the positive fraction and λ is the estimated mean number of target copies per partition. This simple expression assumes an appropriate random-partition model and classification. Concentration then requires partition-volume and dilution information. Intermediate fluorescence, variable partition size, coalescence, and nearly complete positivity compromise inference. A rare-variant detection claim must retain its sample input, background, decision rule, and uncertainty; figures from unmatched conditions cannot rank dPCR against qPCR universally. Reporting with dMIQE preserves the necessary context without proving the model correct. [14,105]

Isothermal amplification is a candidate for targeted detection where its simpler acquisition fits the intended setting. It still requires matrix-specific competence and specificity evidence. Time to positivity is a signal-derived endpoint, not automatically an absolute target quantity. A positive target sequence establishes molecular detection; disease, viable burden, and infectivity remain separate questions. [15,16,54]

### Choose the scale of expression measurement

| Required information | First shortlist | What makes it a good fit | What changes the choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A predefined set of transcript measurements | **RT-qPCR**; **RT-dPCR** for a partition-based quantity model | Targeted follow-up with an explicit calibration or normalization strategy | An unanticipated transcript, isoform, or heterogeneous population is central to the question. [14,104] |
| Broad expression differences in a specimen or defined population | **Bulk RNA-seq** | Transcriptome-scale discovery without requiring each observation to retain its cell of origin | A population average can hide distinct cell states or a change in cellular composition. [20,102] |
| Cell types, state distributions, or rare responding populations | **Single-cell or single-nucleus RNA-seq** | Cell- or nucleus-associated expression profiles | Capture, sampling, multiplets, and the cell-versus-nucleus compartment must fit the question. [20,77] |
| Expression with tissue coordinates | **In situ hybridization or spatial RNA families** | Spatial attribution is part of the biological claim | Targeted coverage, mixed spatial units, and segmentation constrain the result. [44] |

Bulk RNA-seq assigns sequence observations to genes or transcripts and estimates their representation. Library selection and normalization determine which RNA population is represented; a normalized abundance is not automatically an absolute count per cell. Use bulk data when the specimen-level contrast is the intended result, and a cell-resolved route when distribution or composition is itself the question. Targeted confirmation can test selected findings without reproducing the discovery assay's full scope. [102]

Cell and nucleus measurements also need a biological replication plan: thousands of sampled cells from one donor do not become thousands of independent donors. Choose an implementation with evidence for the relevant specimen and recovery of the populations of interest, rather than treating a benchmark from another tissue as a universal ranking. [20,86]

### Sequence, heterogeneity, and epigenetic layers

| Measurement question | Candidates and signal-to-endpoint chain | What must remain conditional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-range DNA linkage, repeats, or variants | **HiFi circular consensus:** incorporation pulses → repeated-read consensus; **nanopore:** ionic current → sequence and variant calls | DNA integrity, calling errors, benchmark context, and software/chemistry version; long reads do not guarantee every accurate call. [17,18] |
| Native RNA isoforms | **Direct RNA sequencing:** nanopore current traces → base and splice assignments | Sequence and isoform calls depend on capture and decoding. Modification-specific claims need separate calibration and validation; a current trace is not itself a modification label. [19] |
| Cellular transcript heterogeneity | **Single-cell/single-nucleus RNA-seq:** barcoded reads and UMIs → gene-by-cell/nucleus counts and normalized expression | Capture, multiplets, sparsity, and normalization; a UMI removes some duplication but cannot recover uncaptured RNA. [20,77] |
| DNA cytosine modification | **Bisulfite-based methods, EM-seq, TAPS:** chemistry-dependent sequence transitions → site fractions, 0–1 or % | Specify which cytosine states the chemistry distinguishes and how conversion is checked. A modification fraction does not measure transcriptional activity. [21,109,115] |
| Selected chromatin feature localization | **CUT&RUN/CUT&Tag:** antibody-localized cleavage/tagmentation → enriched fragments → peaks and enrichment | Antibody specificity, background, normalization, and epitope access qualify localization; enrichment alone does not establish causal regulation. [22,110,111] |
| Same-fiber accessibility and haplotype context | **Fiber-seq:** accessibility-associated labeling plus long-read decoding → phased footprints and modification probabilities | Preserve molecule and labeling context. Co-accessibility on a fiber does not by itself establish enhancer function. [112,113] |

HiFi and nanopore are separated by their physical signals and the contiguity/accuracy requirements they can address. Read-length and accuracy comparisons are implementation- and benchmark-specific. High-molecular-weight DNA and appropriate benchmark genomes matter more to a defensible shortlist than an unqualified read-length claim. Short-read sequencing remains a candidate when the required feature is resolvable in its reads; long-range linkage must be justified by the biological question rather than assumed to be universally preferable. [17,18]

Single-cell sampling is appropriate when an average would hide distinct populations. Its zeros can reflect sampling and biology. The choice of a zero-inflation or other count model should be justified for the measurement chemistry and data, rather than imposed on all single-cell experiments. [114] Cell identity, differential expression, and trajectories are interpretations of counts. Single-nucleus preparation is a specimen variant requiring its own comparability evidence; tissue location needs the spatial route below. [20,77]

For cytosine modification, conventional bisulfite methods do not distinguish 5-methylcytosine from 5-hydroxymethylcytosine in their conventional implementation. EM-seq and TAPS use opposite conversion logic: the former reads unmodified cytosine as thymine, the latter reads modified sites as thymine. [115] Modified and unmodified references must therefore be interpreted against the actual chemistry. A control failure must be interpreted according to the chemistry: the same sequence transition can have different meanings in different methods. [21,109]

Chromatin feature localization, accessibility, and contact organization are separate claims. CUT&RUN/CUT&Tag require suitable recognition and normalization; Fiber-seq retains information along individual chromatin fibers. Select according to the feature or linkage required, then obtain method-specific evidence for the relevant specimen. [22,110,112]

## Protein amount, form, activity, and neighborhood

“Protein level” can mean digested-peptide signal, accessible epitopes, intact molecular forms, or a modified fraction. A **proteoform** is a particular form of a gene product, including sequence and post-translational differences; a **peptidoform** is a particular peptide form. Digestion removes linkage between distant features, so an accurately identified modified peptide does not establish the complete proteoform from which it came. [24,116]

### Start with the protein property you need

| Candidate | Choose it when | Observable → endpoint | Main reason to choose an alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ELISA and related targeted immunoassays** | A known antigen in a defined matrix is the priority and suitable recognition reagents exist | Enzyme-associated optical signal → calibrated amount/concentration, or a defined relative response | Accessible epitopes do not represent the desired total protein or form. Sandwich and competitive formats have different recognition requirements. [117] |
| **Western blot / immunoblot** | Apparent molecular-size context, a cleavage pattern, or target-associated bands help distinguish interpretations | Size-separated immunodetection → band pattern and validated relative abundance | The question requires broad discovery or a calibrated concentration that the method has not established. Band identity, transfer, normalization, and unsaturated detection remain necessary. [118] |
| **Targeted MS: SRM/MRM or PRM** | Preselected peptides or modified peptides provide the needed molecular discrimination | Chromatographic fragment-ion signals → peptide ratios or calibrated amounts | Digestion or peptide-to-protein inference cannot retain the molecular linkage the question requires. [23,119] |
| **Discovery MS: DDA or DIA** | The relevant protein set is not yet fully specified | Peptide spectra and intensities → identifications, protein-group inference, relative abundance | A narrow, predefined measurement with sufficient validation would answer the decision more directly. [116] |
| **Multiplex affinity panels** | Many predefined targets must be compared in the same study | Recognition-associated signals → platform-specific relative or calibrated results | Unlisted targets, altered recognition, or missing molecular-form information are central. [120,121] |

ELISA selects for antigen recognition, whereas an immunoblot adds electrophoretic context. Neither establishes the identity of every detected species simply because an antibody binds. Use evidence appropriate to the antibody's actual application; validation in one format does not automatically transfer to another. [117,118,122]

For targeted MS, **SRM** and **MRM** denote selected/multiple reaction monitoring; **PRM** denotes parallel reaction monitoring. They differ in fragment acquisition, but both require evidence that the measured signal belongs to the specified peptide. Internal-standard design is a separate choice: a mature labeled peptide does not undergo the protein extraction and proteolysis experienced by its endogenous precursor. Protein-level quantification therefore needs a strategy that addresses those steps as well as peptide measurement. [23,119]

The useful shortlist therefore follows the claim: an antigen-associated amount, a size-associated form, a selected peptide quantity, or a broader discovery profile. An agreement test should compare the same intended property; disagreement between epitope and peptide measurements may expose a biological or analytical difference rather than identify a universally inferior platform. [23,118,120,121]

### Discovery and predefined protein panels

In data-dependent acquisition (DDA), selected peptide precursors are fragmented; data-independent acquisition (DIA) fragments ions across predefined mass windows rather than selecting only particular precursors. Tandem Mass Tags (TMT) support isobaric multiplexing. Acquisition mode, labeling strategy, and quantitative analysis are separate choices; TMT is not a synonym for either DDA or DIA. [116,123]

| Candidate and specimen | Principle → observable → endpoint | Main selection tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bottom-up LC-MS/MS**, with DDA or DIA, in lysates/biofluids | Peptide ionization/fragmentation → precursor and fragment signals → peptide identities, protein-group inference, relative abundance | Discovery can extend beyond predefined targets; shared peptides, dynamic range, digestion, and ion suppression limit completeness. [23,116] |
| **TMT/iTRAQ isobaric multiplexing** | Equal-mass tags with distinct reporter fragments → reporter-ion intensities → corrected channel ratios/fold changes | Comparative multiplexing can reduce missingness; co-isolated ions can compress ratios; acquisition strategies that reduce interference have their own sensitivity and speed tradeoffs. [80,123] |
| **SCoPE2 and related single-cell MS** | Low-material peptide analysis with carrier/reference information → reporter ions → relative per-cell protein ratios | Protein heterogeneity differs from transcript heterogeneity; excessive carrier can suppress small signals within finite ion capacity. [80,123] |
| **nanoPOTS (nanodroplet processing in one pot for trace samples)** | Nanodroplet sample preparation with LC-MS → peptide/protein profiles | A separate low-material preparation route; the SCoPE2 carrier/reporter-ion description above does not define nanoPOTS. [124] |
| **Proximity extension assay, PEA**, such as Olink, in biofluids | Dual-antibody recognition links oligonucleotides → PCR/sequencing signal → Normalized Protein eXpression (NPX), a relative log2 scale | Predefined low-abundance targets and limited specimen can favor the approach; epitope access and dual recognition constrain what “quantity” means. [120,125,126] |
| **Modified-aptamer profiling**, such as SomaScan, in biofluids | Target recognition and retained aptamer signal → fluorescence → relative fluorescence units, RFU | Different recognition chemistry and predefined coverage; off-target binding and altered accessible epitopes can mimic amount changes. [121] |

Discovery MS and affinity panels can disagree because they interrogate different properties. An antibody pair needs both epitopes to be accessible; an aptamer may recognize a different state or an off-target. Binding partners, truncation, modifications, or sequence variation can alter affinity signal without changing total protein. MS avoids that specific epitope dependence but introduces peptide recovery, ionization, and protein-inference assumptions. Neither a cross-platform correlation nor a catalog size establishes universal equivalence or superiority. [116,120,121,127]

For MS, target-decoy and false-discovery analysis concern identification, while calibration and normalization concern quantity. They answer different doubts. Isobaric comparisons need isotope correction, loading normalization, and reference-channel context; plex capacity and precision must be tied to the reagent, acquisition method, and study context. Ratios stay relative without an absolute calibration. For PEA, reaction controls and cross-batch bridging support signal integrity; NPX and RFU cannot be relabeled pg/mL by choosing a different reporting unit. [23,80,120,123]

Compare practical requirements for the actual target set and matrix: specimen volume including repeats, recovery, instrument and analysis capacity, and access to follow-up measurements. A predefined affinity panel cannot discover an unlisted target, while a discovery MS experiment still samples only part of the proteome. Neither catalog size nor acquisition speed is a complete measure of usefulness. [116,120,121]

### Functional and specialized protein properties

Activity-based protein profiling (ABPP) examines probe-reactive sites; thermal proteome profiling (TPP) examines thermal-solubility responses. These function-associated measurements require different inference from an abundance measurement. [25,26]

| Required property | Named candidates and endpoint | What the signal cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Probe-reactive sites and competition | **ABPP**, including TOP/isoTOP variants: selective probe labeling/enrichment → site-associated fluorescence or MS ratios | Total enzyme amount or catalytic turnover; access and probe chemistry define the measured subproteome. [25] |
| Thermal-solubility response across proteins | **TPP:** soluble-fraction peptide intensities → fitted melting curves, Tm and ΔTm in °C | A unique direct ligand interaction or functional inhibition; complexes and modifications can also shift thermal behavior. [26,128] |
| Time-dependent molecular neighborhood | **APEX2/TurboID proximity labeling:** localized tagging → enriched protein signals → identifications/relative enrichment | Direct binding, fixed universal radius, or functional necessity. [28,129,130] |
| Maturation and proteolytic cleavage | **N-terminal TAILS:** original-terminus enrichment → peptide positions and relative abundance | Every cleavage substrate or a symmetric account of C-terminal biology. [131] |
| Accessible surface glycoprotein subset | **Cell Surface Capture, CSC:** glycan-associated enrichment → peptide assignments and relative signals | A complete surfaceome or universal proof of orientation; inaccessible and nonglycosylated targets can be missed. [27] |
| Site-specific modification fraction | **PTM occupancy measurements:** modified/unmodified-state evidence → fraction or % | Occupancy from modified signal alone; the denominator and treatment behavior are essential. [132] |
| Glycan linked to peptide/site | **Intact glycopeptide MS**, with EThcD/stepped-HCD options → joint assignments and signal abundance | Unqualified complete linkage or calibrated abundance when fragmentation and assignment evidence are inadequate. [133] |
| Intact forms or preserved assemblies | **Top-down, middle-down, native MS:** intact/larger-fragment ion spectra → masses, identities, distributions; ion mobility/CIU adds structural proxies | Complete recovery of every form or a direct solution structure from gas-phase behavior. [24,134,135] |
| Emerging single-molecule sequence-related information | **Protein nanopore measurement:** blockade-current patterns → model-decoded sequence-related information | Mature standardized protein sequencing or validated replacement of established families. [136] |

ABPP is useful when validated probe chemistry can examine a reactive site or competition at that site. No-probe and competition controls help distinguish labeling background from the intended interaction. TPP measures thermal-solubility responses without requiring a modified test ligand, but a shifted protein can reflect direct engagement or an indirect change in protein state. Inspect curve quality and use complementary measurements when direct binding is the claim. [25,26,128]

Proximity labeling integrates a neighborhood over a labeling interval. APEX2 and TurboID differ in chemistry, temporal context, intervention effects, and background accumulation. Enzyme-absent and compartment-reference evidence help identify endogenous or ubiquitous labeling. The spatial extent of labeling depends on its chemistry and biological context; it is not one fixed radius. Choose this route when a neighborhood is the required evidence, and add binding or functional measurements when the claim requires them. [28,130,137]

Specialized subproteome methods answer questions broad protein abundance cannot. Terminomics depends on blocking/depletion and recovery; CSC depends on accessible extracellular glycan chemistry. Occupancy needs both modified and unmodified information: the same fourfold modified-signal change could accompany very different starting fractions. Low-occupancy noise can produce impossible estimates, which calls for uncertainty treatment rather than a biological story. Released-glycan profiling below is useful for composition, but loses the original protein/site link that intact glycopeptides aim to retain. [27,31,131,132]

Select intact and native measurements for the molecular linkage or assembly information they retain. Emerging nanopore approaches need application-specific evidence before being treated as alternatives to established protein assays. ProForma and PEFF help encode forms and modifications for reuse; they cannot restore linkage that an experiment did not measure. [24,135,136,138,139]

## Chemical composition is not metabolic flux

A metabolite pool can grow because production rises or consumption falls; a small pool can turn over rapidly. “More metabolite” and “more pathway activity” therefore require different evidence. Also distinguish an analytical feature, a molecular formula, a structural assignment, and a calibrated amount. Accurate mass alone cannot distinguish structural isomers with the same formula. [29,32]

### Composition and identity

Liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) combines separation with mass-selective detection. Adding ion mobility in LC-IMS-MS provides another analytical dimension. Isotopically nonstationary metabolic flux analysis (INST-MFA), discussed below, instead fits changing label patterns to a network model. [29,140]

| Need and specimen | First candidates; observable and units | Discriminating limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A specified chemical concentration in a biological matrix | **Isotope-dilution LC-MS/MS:** chromatographic ion-current areas/internal-standard ratio → calibrated concentration, such as ng/mL | Recovery, selectivity, ion suppression/enhancement, and standard behavior must be demonstrated in that matrix. [141,142] |
| Broad small-molecule discovery in fluids or extracts | **LC-IMS-MS:** retention, m/z, mobility/collision cross section, intensity → relative feature matrix | Feature processing, artifacts, and annotation limit identity; authentic standards support stronger confirmation. [29,143] |
| Rapid lipid class/composition profiling | **Shotgun MDMS-SL:** direct-infusion/class-associated spectra → relative or calibrated quantities, such as nmol/mg protein | Isotope overlap, crowding, and unequal response; basic spectra do not locate all double bonds or positional arrangements. [30,144] |
| Additional lipid/less-polar-metabolite separation | **SFC-MS**, or suitable LC alternatives: retention-associated ion signals → identities and relative/calibrated quantities | Establish separation and response for the compounds in question. A successful separation in one lipid matrix does not establish general isomer resolution. [145] |
| Released-glycan fingerprint in fluids/products | **xCGE-LIF:** fluorescence electropherogram → calibrated migration/glucose units and relative peaks | Migration references support assignments; released glycans no longer retain their original protein/site linkage. [31,146] |
| Released-glycan composition/fragment constraints | **MALDI-TOF**, including permethylated variants: m/z and fragments → composition and sequence constraints | Derivatization or group loss can create artifacts; mass does not establish every linkage or molar quantity. [31,147] |

Targeted isotope dilution is attractive when calibrated quantity and chemical selectivity are central. Adding a suitable internal standard before extraction can track preparation as well as ionization, but near-similar chemistry does not guarantee identical response. Recovery and matrix effects are distinct: post-extraction additions examine response in the extract, not how much target survived preparation. Reference suitability and commutability also matter. A matrix-effect acceptance criterion must be specified and justified for the assay; no universal threshold is implied here. [141,142]

Untargeted LC-IMS-MS begins with analytical features. Blanks and pooled quality-control material help distinguish contamination and drift from specimen differences. Retention, fragmentation, mobility, and authentic standards add evidence for identity. Report the annotation framework and the evidence behind each assignment; an annotation-confidence category is not a quantitative-validation result. [29,143,148,149]

Shotgun lipidomics favors rapid class-oriented analysis but trades away chromatographic discrimination. Class standards support quantity and isotope correction addresses overlapping species; neither grants unsupported stereochemistry. SFC-MS is a candidate where a particular configuration can resolve mixtures missed by simpler methods, but its specialized equipment and chemical coverage must fit the specimen. A reported rapid separation is not proof that every isomer is resolved. [30,144,145]

For glycans, decide whether the question concerns a released profile or a particular protein/site. Migration ladders and discriminating references support xCGE-LIF; selective enzymatic comparisons can test linkage hypotheses. Release chemistry can miss certain substrates, including substrates with core glycan features that are poorly addressed by PNGase F, so a universal required-reagent claim is unwarranted. MALDI composition and fragmentation complement electrophoretic patterns, while derivatization completeness and labile-group preservation qualify the result. O-glycan coverage is thinner than N-glycan coverage. [31]

### Rates, energy metabolism, and isotope history

| Question | Candidate and endpoint | Information needed to defend the inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How does material flow through an intracellular network? | **Isotope tracing/INST-MFA:** isotopologue time courses plus pools → model-derived flux, for example moles/cell/time | Tracer enrichment, natural-abundance correction, pool sizes, atom mapping, compartments, and model identifiability. [32,140] |
| What fraction was newly synthesized? | **Deuterium-water synthesis measurements:** incorporation evidence → fractional synthesis with a defined time basis | Labeling and compartment/model assumptions; enrichment alone is not a universal synthesis rate. [150] |
| What is respiration or acidification doing? | **Extracellular flux/high-resolution respirometry:** optical O₂/pH signals or electrode current → OCR, ECAR, corrected PER/glycoPER | Biological normalization, buffering, background, pathway response, and respiratory CO₂ accounting. [33,151] |
| Does a purified molecule retain an origin-associated isotope pattern? | **SNIF-NMR/isotope-ratio MS applications:** calibrated spectra → position-specific or bulk isotope ratios | Appropriate references and purity; origin/adulteration is an inference, distinct from dynamic tracer flux. [152] |

INST-MFA accommodates changing isotope patterns; it does not remove every biochemical or network assumption. Label scrambling, continued reaction before measurement, omitted compartments, and multiple equally good model fits can make a flux unidentifiable. A defensible endpoint includes uncertainty for the parameters the data actually constrain. Time-series sampling and modeling expertise are substantial selection requirements, unlike a one-time abundance measurement. [32,153,154]

Respiration and acidification are different quantities. Common platform outputs include OCR in pmol O₂/min and ECAR in milli-pH units/min; biological normalization is an additional choice. ECAR is not a molar glycolytic rate: buffer calibration gives proton efflux, and respiratory CO₂ contributes acidification that must be accounted for before a glycolytic interpretation. Multiwell optical systems and continuous electrode respirometry serve different practical and time-course needs; neither reconstructs all intracellular fluxes. Lactate, uptake/secretion, or tracer evidence can supply complementary constraints. [33,151,155]

Natural-isotope fingerprints examine a molecule’s history rather than its current pathway rate. Origin or adulteration claims depend on suitable reference material and the specificity of the observed pattern. An isotope match alone does not establish unique geographic origin. [152]

## Cellular health needs more than one proxy

Define viability operationally before selecting a “viability assay.” Metabolically active material, intact membranes, ongoing division, and capacity for a specialized function are separate properties. A treatment can depress metabolism without killing cells, or cause membrane injury after an earlier metabolic response. Concentration-response midpoints inherit those distinctions: half-inhibition of ATP signal is not automatically a proven lethal concentration. [34,35]

### Count cells when cell number is the question

A metabolic proxy can be useful for screening, but cell number, net growth, and passage through a cell-cycle state are distinct endpoints. Specify whether the desired quantity is cells in the measured field or volume, accumulation relative to a starting population, or the fraction undergoing DNA synthesis. [34,156]

| Required property | Candidate route | What to retain in the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell number | Image-based or cytometric counting with an appropriate sampling denominator | Object/event definition, aggregates and debris, sampled area or volume, and any viability classification. [157] |
| Net population growth | Counts referenced to starting and comparison populations | Observation interval and baseline growth; net accumulation combines division and loss. [156] |
| DNA synthesis or cell-cycle state | EdU/BrdU incorporation; DNA-content cytometry where appropriate | Label interval or DNA-content classification, parent population, and the distinction between state occupancy and completed division. [157,158] |
| Metabolic competence | ATP, tetrazolium, or other validated metabolic readouts | Per-cell response can change, so calibrate the relationship needed for the intended inference. [34] |

Growth-rate inhibition metrics can help distinguish drug-response differences caused by baseline division rate from differences in response. They require suitable starting and comparison measurements and a model that fits the observed growth. They are derived population endpoints, not direct measurements of a death mechanism. [156]

### Metabolism, membrane injury, division, and death markers

| Candidate and specimen | Observable → endpoint | Supported property and main competing explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ATP-dependent luminescence**, including lytic cellular implementations | Luciferase-associated light in relative light units → normalized response or calibrated ATP quantity | ATP-associated cellular material; ATP per cell and reporter interference can change without corresponding survival loss. [34] |
| **MTT/related tetrazolium reduction** | Formazan-associated absorbance → relative reduction/response | Reductive activity; nutrients, pH, and reductase state affect signal independently of cell number. [34] |
| **LDH release** into supernatant | Coupled enzyme absorbance → relative/% release with background and maximum-release context | Membrane leakage; reaction inhibition, compound color, or enzyme decay can distort the response. [159,160] |
| **Annexin V, TUNEL, cleaved-caspase measurements** | Marker-associated fluorescence or immunodetection → positive fraction/relative signal | Respectively membrane asymmetry, DNA fragmentation, or cleavage evidence; one marker does not establish every feature of a death pathway. [35] |
| **Calcein-AM retention** | Cell-associated fluorescence → fraction/intensity | Retention-associated live-cell proxy; transport of the calcein-AM precursor can reduce accumulation in MDR1-expressing cells, so low fluorescence alone does not establish nonviability. [161]  |
| **EdU/BrdU incorporation** | Nascent-DNA label detection by imaging/cytometry → positive fraction during the interval | DNA synthesis during labeling; neither counts completed divisions, and nonreplicative synthesis may need to be excluded. [250] [158] |

Cell-free blanks, vehicle baselines, and characterized response controls establish different aspects of signal range and assay competence. For LDH, spontaneous and maximum release define the denominator; for Annexin, calcium-dependent and membrane-compromised comparisons address distinct interpretations. Death-marker timing matters. A disagreement between ATP and LDH is a reason to examine metabolic suppression, response stage, and interference rather than assign a death mechanism from the disagreement alone. [35,159,160]

Sample reuse can determine the shortlist. Lytic ATP assays consume the measured cells; LDH supernatant sampling can leave a cellular layer for follow-up. EdU and BrdU are interventions as well as labels. EdU detection avoids the DNA-denaturation step used in conventional BrdU immunodetection, but incorporation can still perturb cells. Evaluate label exposure and compatibility with later endpoints; F-ara-EdU is an alternative supported in particular experimental settings, not a universal substitute. [34,159,162,250]

### Mechanics, electrophysiology, and composite trajectories

**Traction force microscopy, TFM**, observes reference displacements and reconstructs a stress field, in Pa or kPa, using calibrated mechanics and an inverse model. A tension-released reference and physical calibration qualify the inference; noise, regularization, and geometry can change estimated forces. Choose it for mechanical traction, rather than infer force from an adhesion-associated optical or impedance signal. A two-dimensional reconstruction does not automatically extend to three-dimensional mechanics or identify the responsible molecular adhesion complex from stress alone. [36,163]

**Patch clamp** measures transmembrane electrical behavior, including channel currents in pA or nA. **Microelectrode arrays, MEAs**, record extracellular voltage fluctuations and derive firing, burst, and synchrony statistics. The former can answer a channel/single-cell question; the latter adds network and longitudinal context. Reference/background and characterized electrical-response evidence test the signal's origin. Coupling, leakage, drift, filtering, and multiunit assignment limit interpretation, while extracellular synchrony does not establish a particular synaptic mechanism or whole-tissue maturity. Calcium imaging is a complement with different spatial and temporal sampling. [37,39]

**Real-time cellular impedance** converts electrical magnitude/phase into a platform-specific Cell Index. Adhesion, spreading, coverage, rounding, detachment, and death can all influence its trajectory. Cell-free and vehicle trajectories address baseline, but independent count, division, or death evidence is needed to separate biological causes. Its useful property is a longitudinal composite response and its timing, rather than a universally calibrated viable-cell number. Environmental and seeding artifacts can alter the trace. [38,164]

The tandem **mRFP-GFP-LC3 reporter** helps distinguish autophagy-associated compartments, but a static image does not by itself measure flux through the pathway. **Nano-flow cytometry** examines extracellular-vesicle-associated particles through scatter and fluorescence; particle identity and count require appropriate calibration and discrimination from other particles. These are distinct measurement problems and need their own validation. [165,166]

### Broad response signatures and intervention attribution

**Cell Painting** turns multichannel images into segmented objects and morphology features: shape, texture, intensity, and spatial organization. Vehicle and characterized-response profiles make comparisons interpretable; density, staining, illumination, autofluorescence, and severe death morphology can dominate the vector. Similarity to a reference supports a response hypothesis, not direct target identification. Fixed-cell imaging, storage, and computation affect follow-up and throughput. [40,167]

**Perturb-seq/CROP-seq** link intervention identifiers to single-cell transcript counts. Non-targeting references, intervention assignment, and biological replication are necessary to interpret response differences. **PerturbSci-Kinetics** adds new-versus-preexisting RNA information to support model-based synthesis/degradation estimates. Successful editing, an assigned perturbation, and a downstream molecular mechanism remain distinct claims. [41,168,169]

## Cells, coordinates, and spatial attribution

### Choose the imaging mode before the marker panel

| Required information | Starting family | Deciding tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morphology or marker distribution in a thin specimen | **Transmitted-light or widefield fluorescence imaging** | Decide whether the contrast identifies the intended structure and whether out-of-focus signal compromises the endpoint. [42] |
| Optical sections through a thicker specimen | **Confocal fluorescence imaging** | Rejection of out-of-focus light improves sectioning; light exposure, signal collection, and scan timing still constrain the measurement. [42] |
| A trajectory in the same cells | **Live time-lapse imaging**, using an appropriate optical mode | Follow events over time while assessing phototoxicity, focus drift, tracking errors, and changes introduced by observation. [170] |
| Marker distribution in fixed tissue | **Immunofluorescence or IHC**, extended to multiplex methods if needed | Preservation, recognition specificity, background, and segmentation determine what the apparent pattern means. [45,122] |

Live versus fixed, widefield versus confocal, and low- versus high-plex are different decisions. A live-cell movie is useful when the ordering or duration of events matters; a fixed measurement can support a state comparison without revealing the path taken to reach it. Confocal sectioning does not by itself validate a label or establish molecular interaction. Choose effective resolution, temporal sampling, and specimen preservation for the biological feature that must be distinguished. [42,122,170]

Imaging mass cytometry (IMC) and multiplexed ion beam imaging by time of flight (MIBI-TOF) map antibody-associated metal signals; cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) reconstructs density, sometimes followed by subtomogram averaging (STA). Choose the biological unit first. A cytometer event, localized transcript, spatial bin, and segmented cell are different units. Detection and assignment are separate operations: even a correctly measured signal can be attributed to the wrong cell. Small sampling spacing, precise localization, and effective cell-level resolution are also different properties. [44,45,171,172]

| Question and material | Candidate families; raw signal and endpoint | Selection boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which suspended cells/particles have particular optical phenotypes? | **Conventional/spectral flow cytometry:** scatter/emission → compensated/unmixed event-by-marker intensities, frequencies, median fluorescence | Efficient event profiling loses tissue architecture; intensities are generally arbitrary units without calibration. Pulse-shape variants add optical particle-phenotype information. [43,157] |
| Where are predefined transcripts in tissue? | **smFISH/optical-code families**, including MERFISH/MERSCOPE, Xenium, CosMx: image stacks → decoded coordinates/counts | Panel coverage, optical crowding, misdecoding, and segmentation qualify detected/assigned molecules. [173,174] |
| Where is broader transcriptomic variation? | **Sequencing-based spatial RNA**, including DBiT-seq, Visium HD, Stereo-seq, Seq-Scope: spatially barcoded reads → counts/bin or assigned cell | Discovery breadth can favor this route; mixed bins, diffusion, and deconvolution limit cell attribution. [44,175,176] |
| Where are predefined protein markers? | **IMC/MIBI-TOF:** metal-tag ion-count images; **cyclic fluorescence**, including PhenoCycler/CODEX: marker images → segmented patterns/neighborhoods | Antibody recognition remains central; metal readouts avoid fluorescence background but add isotope/ion artifacts. [45,171,177] |
| What is tissue or cellular architecture? | **Histological/volumetric imaging:** brightfield or fluorescence images → calibrated morphology/dimensions | Preservation and clearing can alter dimensions, lipids, and fluorescence; improved optical access is not proof of faithful preservation. [178] |
| What organization needs finer structural evidence? | **STORM/PALM/STED:** localized or spatially restricted fluorescence → reconstructed structures; **cryo-ET/STA:** electron projections → density | Label density/photophysics constrain optical structure; near-atomic averaging claims apply to averaged structures, not every raw tomogram. [46,172] |
| Can metabolic spectra discriminate tissue? | **REIMS:** aerosol-associated mass signals → classifier output | This is a tissue-discrimination endpoint, not a spatial antibody map; a universal clinical accuracy estimate is absent. [179] |

In cytometry, single-label references support overlap correction or unmixing; unstained specimens characterize background; fluorescence-minus-one information supports population boundaries. FMO is not itself numerical compensation. Spreading and cell-type-dependent autofluorescence remain even after modeled overlap is removed, especially for dim/rare populations. Preserve the parent population and gating model with every percentage. [43,157]

Spatial RNA comparisons should ask whether a targeted optical panel covers the biological question and whether broader discovery justifies mixed-coordinate inference. Negative probes and blank decoding entries examine different errors in optical methods; spatial leakage and registration evidence matter for sequencing-based methods. Segmentation participates in endpoint formation: changing a cell boundary can change its assigned expression and neighbors. Reference profiles and model assumptions in deconvolution do not turn a mixed bin into a directly measured pure cell. [44,173,174]

IMC and MIBI-TOF consume material during acquisition, which can constrain subsequent measurements. Their ion signals remain recognition- and tissue-dependent, rather than automatically calibrated concentrations. Histological context can support registration, but adjacent sections do not contain identical cells. Assess preservation and registration according to the joint biological claim; no universal speed ranking against cyclic fluorescence is warranted here. [45,178,180]

Multimodal comparisons need explicit provenance. Same-cell co-measurement, adjacent sections, matched populations, and computational integration support different claims about joint states. DBiT-seq illustrates co-mapping of molecular layers within a defined spatial workflow. Co-localized ligand and receptor signals can motivate a communication hypothesis, but secretion, activation, and causation require additional evidence. [44,88]

## Immune recognition, response, and effector function

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) are a common specimen for the cellular immune measurements discussed here. An immune assay may ask whether recognition occurs, whether a mediator response follows, whether granules are released, or whether a target is killed. Protection and clinical effect are further claims. Antibody use as a detection reagent does not make every protein assay an immune-function measurement. [48,49,50]

### Cellular response and cell identity

| Required property | First candidates; endpoint and specimen | Interpretation and controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency of detected secretion | **ELISpot/FluoroSpot** in PBMCs/immune populations: spot images → spot-forming units per well or normalized input | Background, competence, specificity, cell preservation, and image classification matter; a spot does not supply complex lineage identity. [48,181] |
| Phenotype-linked intracellular mediator response | **Intracellular cytokine staining, ICS:** event fluorescence/scatter → cytokine-positive % within a stated parent population | Background, FMO boundaries, compromised-cell signal, and processing effects qualify response; systemic secretion concentration is unmeasured. [48,157,182] |
| Detectable receptor recognition | **Peptide–MHC tetramers/dextramers:** reagent-associated fluorescence → positive lineage fraction | Scaffold/specificity evidence and affinity/avidity constrain recovery; positive binding is not competence, negative binding can miss low-affinity cells. [47] |
| Granule exocytosis versus successful killing | **CD107a** → degranulating fraction; **cytotoxicity/ADCC** → distinct target-loss/lysis endpoint | Background and target-loss references address specificity/range; resistant targets and effector context can separate exocytosis from lysis. [49,50] |
| Receptor clone composition | **Adaptive Immune Receptor Repertoire (AIRR) sequencing:** rearrangement reads/annotation → clonotype fractions and diversity | Retain specimen, processing, and annotation provenance. The endpoint describes repertoire composition; claims about antigen recognition or effector function require corresponding measurements. [183] |

ELISpot and ICS can be complementary rather than interchangeable. ELISpot accumulates extracellular secretion footprints; ICS observes intracellular mediator accumulation and can connect it to a phenotype. Their temporal properties, preservation effects, and background differ. Spot count per well and frequency per input population need explicit, different denominators; a normalization factor should never be inferred from an unrelated example. Similarly, a cytokine-positive fraction among memory CD8 cells differs from a fraction among all PBMCs. [48,182]

FluoroSpot and multi-marker ICS can examine combined responses, but **polyfunctionality** depends on the mediator set, thresholds, and assignment. More detected functions are not automatically evidence of better protection. Tetramer and higher-valency multimer differences concern detectable recognition and avidity; ADCC is a separate functional measurement involving antibody, target, and effector cells. [47,48,50]

### Fluids, antibodies, and pathway function

Soluble-mediator multiplex assays measure fluid-level cytokine/chemokine profiles rather than the secreting cell. Bead arrays such as Luminex associate reporter fluorescence with bead identity; electrochemiluminescence such as MSD uses electrically generated light; PEA converts dual recognition into a nucleic-acid signal. Standards can yield concentration, such as pg/mL, when validation supports that conversion. NPX remains relative. Matrix inhibition, recognition cross-reactivity, and batch behavior prevent universal platform superiority claims; internal reaction controls cannot replace target-specific selectivity. [120,126,185]

**Anti-drug antibody, ADA, assays** assess binding reactivity toward a therapeutic product. Screening, specificity confirmation, characterization, and neutralization testing address different questions. Report assay-specific status and titer separately from functional inhibition. Residual drug, soluble target, and pre-existing reactivity can alter detection; drug tolerance and specificity therefore affect the observed incidence. Interference-management steps require evidence that the intended antibody response remains detectable. [51,186,187]

**Surrogate interaction-inhibition assays**, such as sVNT, measure one molecular interaction. Steric binding can inhibit that interaction without proving broader biological neutralization; functionally relevant antibodies outside the represented epitope/mechanism can be missed. **Functional neutralization** measures reduction of activity in a selected biological system and reports a defined titer. Neither binding, surrogate inhibition, nor in vitro function alone establishes protection in people. The microbial comparison below makes the same distinction between a measured response and its interpretation. [52,188]

**CH50/AH50** assess complement-pathway function rather than the concentration of each component. CH50 examines classical-pathway function and AH50 examines alternative-pathway function. Interpret the pattern using the assay’s format, units, reference information, and specimen requirements. A functional abnormality can guide further investigation but does not by itself identify the defective component. [53]

## Microbial presence, quantity, and capability

Specify which biological capability matters. A sequence can persist after death; intact membranes can remain after viability is lost; an organism can be viable yet not grow in the selected assay. Physical viral particles, genomes, and infection-associated units are different quantities. Selecting a fast detection method is reasonable only when molecular detection is the required claim. [54,55,189]

| Required claim | Candidate route and endpoint | What remains unestablished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target molecules occur | Targeted amplification or sequence assignment → target signal/concentration | Living organisms, anatomical source, infectivity, or causal disease. [13,189] |
| Growth is detectable in this system | Culture-associated enumeration → CFU per specified mass/volume or growth detection | Culturability under the selected conditions, rather than recovery of every viable organism. Report colony-forming units as the operational endpoint. [54] |
| Target signal is associated with membrane exclusion | **PMA/EMA viability PCR:** amplification signal → calibrated genome/CFU equivalents | Metabolism, replication, or infectivity; CFU equivalents are not directly counted colonies. [54,189] |
| Infection-associated activity occurs in the represented host-cell system | Plaque/infectious-dose endpoints → PFU or statistically estimated TCID50 with denominator | Total particles/genomes or human transmission risk; no universal PFU–TCID50 conversion is supplied. [55] |
| Antibody-containing material reduces that activity | **PRNT or reporter-surrogate neutralization**, including PsVNA → defined reduction titer | Universal equivalence among systems or guaranteed in vivo protection. [190] |
| Recognizable parasite structures are present | **Kato-Katz:** eggs/g feces; **IMS-IFA:** recognized cysts/oocysts per specified water volume | Adult-worm counts, viable burden, or human infectivity. [56,191] |
| A fungal optical phenotype suggests identity | Chromogenic color/morphology → presumptive classification | Confirmatory identification may be needed because related organisms can share a phenotype. [192] |
| Circulating microbial DNA fragments are detected | **Plasma mcfDNA sequencing** → molecules/µL or background-relative statistic | Living microbial burden, source location, or active sepsis. [193] |

Competence, recovery, matrix background, and detection capability determine how informative non-detection is. In membrane-integrity PCR, membrane-state and amplification controls address different errors. For functional viral measurements, report the represented host-cell system and activity endpoint; the result is conditional on that assay context. [54,55]

Microscopic counts need the examined mass or original water volume and a recovery estimate appropriate to the method. Fungal color or morphology can provide presumptive identification, with confirmation required when related organisms share the phenotype. Plasma mcfDNA provides a fragment-based measurement whose positivity may persist longer than culture; it should not be interpreted as a direct count of living organisms. [56,191,192,193]

### Susceptibility and biofilms

The biofilm endpoint MBEC means minimum biofilm eradication concentration in the represented recovery model; XTT is a tetrazolium-reduction readout, while crystal violet measures retained biomass. [58,194]

The **minimum inhibitory concentration, MIC**, measures planktonic growth inhibition in concentration units, commonly µg/mL or mg/L. “Susceptible” or “resistant” adds a drug–organism–interpretive-framework judgment; it is not part of the raw growth endpoint. Clinical categorization requires the applicable organism, agent, method, and interpretive-framework version. Resistance-marker detection asks a different molecular question and is not a substitute for observed phenotype. [57,58]

For a biofilm, distinguish **recoverable survival**, **retained biomass**, and **metabolic reduction**. MBEC concerns a concentration associated with no detected surviving/recoverable growth in a model. Crystal-violet absorbance includes matrix and live/dead material. XTT reduction is a metabolic proxy. Reduced XTT with retained biomass can therefore be a coherent result, while reduced staining does not establish eradication. Recovery and background/interference evidence serve different purposes, and cross-laboratory MBEC comparability remains unresolved. MIC and MBEC should not be converted using a general factor. [58,194,195]

### Composition, absolute scale, and community function

Marker sequencing, including short/full-length 16S, turns reads into taxonomic assignments and relative fractions. Full-length information can improve discrimination in a particular context without removing recovery, amplification, reference-database, or marker-copy biases. A larger fraction of one taxon can result from another taxon's decline; the first taxon's absolute abundance need not rise. Reference-scaled quantities add information about specimen scale but remain estimates rather than exact cells per gram by definition. [59,196,197]

Community function requires another measurement layer. Gene inventories, sampled transcripts, metabolite pools, and process rates describe different properties. **F420-associated fluorescence** can identify a cofactor-associated phenotype in particular organisms; it is not a methane-production rate. Select a rate endpoint when realized community activity is the claim. [32,198,199]

## Tissue, organism, and behavioral measurements

Short-circuit current (Isc) and transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) are distinct electrical endpoints; intrinsic clearance (CLint) is a derived removal-capacity measure. Choose a tissue, organoid, or organism model for the function and exposure it can represent, then specify the measurement. A model name or larger biological scale does not, by itself, establish predictive validity. [60,61]

| Construct and system | Candidate measurement and endpoint | Main alternative explanation or translation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epithelial transport/barrier behavior in tissue or monolayers | **Transepithelial electrophysiology:** voltage/current → Isc (µA/cm²) or TEER (Ω·cm²) | Net electrogenic transport and resistance are different; deterioration or shunts can mimic biological dysfunction, and current alone does not identify an ion/transporter. [60] |
| Drug removal in a liver model | **LC-MS/MS concentration trajectories:** parent/metabolite signal → half-life and cell-normalized intrinsic clearance | Nonbiological loss and concentration changes can mimic metabolism; model clearance is not measured whole-body clearance. [61,200] |
| Neural network activity in cerebral organoids | **MEA:** extracellular voltage → event/field activity, bursts, synchrony | Regional sampling, tissue state, and coupling qualify silence/activity; coordinated activity does not establish whole-brain maturity. [201,202] |
| Acute survival in fish embryos | **FET:** defined lethal observations → mortality and exposure-dependent LC50 | Species, developmental stage, interval, and measured exposure qualify the result; absence of acute lethality does not exclude chronic effects. [203] |
| Lineage differentiation in human iPSC models | Trilineage assays, including **ReproTracker:** markers/morphology → relative differentiation outcomes | Limited metabolism and selected lineages constrain translation; altered markers are not direct fetal outcomes. [204] |
| Spatial learning in rodents | **Morris water maze:** trajectories → latency (s), path length (m), search-region % | Sensory/motor limits, floating, wall following, and physiological state can alter performance independently of memory. [62] |
| Attention/inhibition in rodents | **5-CSRTT/CPT variants:** timestamps → reaction time, omissions, premature responses | Motivation, movement, and processing contribute; response categories are endpoints before being cognitive labels. [205,206] |
| Locomotor phenotype in C. elegans | Automated tracking → velocity, bend frequency (Hz), posture/path features | Segmentation, identity swaps, stage, and illness affect tracks; phenotypic rescue does not prove molecular engagement. [207,208] |

Barrier measurements and chemical passage answer related but distinct questions. TEER is not whole-body absorption, while tissue Isc can mask cellular heterogeneity. Clearance measurements need metabolic competence, analytical calibration, mass balance, and nonbiological-loss evidence. Their units must retain the stated time basis and cell or tissue normalization; an undocumented change between minutes and hours prevents quantitative comparison. Translating CLint through physiological modeling is another inference layer, not a unit conversion into measured human clearance. [60,61,65]

Behavioral selection needs **construct validity**: evidence that the observed pattern supports the intended interpretation. Compare escape latency with search bias and sensory/motor reference performance when investigating memory; do not call every omission an attentional deficit or every faster response a cognitive improvement. Automated tracking improves recording but does not remove motivation, physiological, or image-analysis confounding. Numerous time points in one animal remain repeated observations of that animal. Training, space, and analysis demands also distinguish behavioral tasks from rapid screens. [62,86,205]

## Pharmacology: engagement, exposure, and hazard

A screening hit is a starting hypothesis. Primary activity, artifact counter-screening, independent confirmation, cellular engagement, disposition, and hazard assessment each ask a different question. **EC50**, **IC50**, **KD**, and degradation **DC50** describe different endpoints; a concentration-response midpoint becomes meaningful only with its property, system, and analysis model. [7,63,209]

### Engagement and actual consequence

**CETSA** assesses a target’s thermal-solubility response using soluble-protein detection. Depending on the format, the endpoint is a temperature-response parameter or an isothermal concentration response. It supplies cellular-context evidence related to engagement, rather than a direct measurement of biochemical efficacy. Conventional CETSA includes heating and separation of soluble material, so sample consumption matters. Interpret the result with controls and complementary binding or functional evidence appropriate to the target. [26,63,128,257]

**NanoBRET target-engagement assays** measure competition between a test compound and a fluorescent tracer at a NanoLuc-tagged target. Tracer displacement reduces the binding-associated BRET signal. Other BRET formats measure proximity and require a different interpretation. Specify the construct, tracer, signal direction, and interference controls. In targeted degradation, a proximity or competition response does not supply a DC50: that endpoint requires a defined protein-abundance response. [209,251]

Galectin-associated fluorescent puncta supply a different proxy: endosomal membrane damage exposing luminal glycans. Damage, uptake, cytosolic availability, translation, and productive delivery are separate endpoints. A strong punctate response can accompany catastrophic injury rather than useful delivery. Viability and translation-related evidence are necessary when the claim goes beyond damage. [210]

### ADME and exposure-related measurements

| Needed property | Supported candidates and endpoint | Essential evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passive membrane diffusion | **PAMPA/PAMPA-BBB:** concentration passage → effective permeability, cm/s or log permeability | Recovery, ionization, retention, and reference context; active transport, paracellular passage, and metabolism are absent. [211] |
| Passage through represented cellular transport biology | **Caco-2/MDCK-MDR1:** directional appearance → Papp (cm/s) and efflux ratio | Barrier integrity, mass balance, free exposure, and relevant transporter evidence; asymmetry alone does not identify one transporter. [64,212] |
| Unbound plasma fraction | **Equilibrium dialysis** with concentration measurement; mobility-shift alternatives for selected modalities → fu, a fraction | Equilibrium, recovery, volume/matrix behavior, and modality-specific adsorption/leakage. [213,214] |
| Pathway contribution and interaction potential | **CYP phenotyping/transporter assays:** turnover or probe accumulation → fm, CLint, IC50/Ki | Biological competence, selective inhibition, background, and effective unbound concentration. [65,215] |
| Measured exposure to support further modeling | **Targeted LC-MS/MS** → calibrated concentration/time information | Matrix validation and analytical performance; accurate concentration does not by itself validate PK/PD or PBPK prediction. [141] |

High passive permeability with poor cellular passage suggests an additional cellular constraint but does not identify it uniquely. Likewise, material missing from receiver measurements can reflect retention or loss rather than poor transport. For protein binding, dialysis suitability for small molecules cannot simply be transferred to oligonucleotides; mobility-shift or ultracentrifugation alternatives need modality-specific recovery evidence. Low fu informs disposition models without directly determining tissue distribution. [211,212,214]

Recombinant enzyme measurements, selective-inhibition experiments, and physiological extrapolation answer successive questions. Applying guidance thresholds requires the intended use, denominator, and document version. A complete pharmacology assessment also needs exposure, efficacy, selectivity, and relevant safety evidence beyond the illustrative measurements compared here. [65,215]

### Choose the hazard rather than a generic safety label

The **Ames** endpoint concerns mutation-associated restoration of a bacterial phenotype; **cytokinesis-block micronucleus** measurements concern chromosome breakage/segregation-associated structures in a divided-cell context. Background, characterized positive mechanisms, metabolic competence, and toxicity/proliferation evidence determine whether the biological system could reveal the hazard. Bacterial toxicity can hide mutation, and apoptotic objects can resemble micronuclei. These are complementary hazard questions, not interchangeable predictions of cancer risk. [66,216]

**hERG electrophysiology** measures channel current and its inhibition. Recording quality, effective exposure, and reference responses qualify the endpoint; additional evidence is needed to assess clinical arrhythmia risk. Like the genetic-toxicity assays above, it addresses a particular hazard rather than establishing that a compound is safe in every respect. [39,217]

## Clinical measurements and their decision context

Clinical selection adds an intended decision and patient population to the measurement claim. Separate three questions: is the analyte measured reliably; does the result identify the relevant clinical state; and does using the result improve the decision or outcome? The ACCE framework makes these distinctions for genetic tests. Diagnostic sensitivity and specificity concern classification against a clinical reference, whereas detection limits and analytical selectivity concern measurement performance. A reference interval describes a reference population; a clinical decision limit serves a specified diagnostic or management purpose. [67,82,254]

| Clinical question and specimen | Developed candidate; observable → endpoint | Key selection/confirmation distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Myocardial-injury-associated protein in serum/plasma | **High-sensitivity cTnI/cTnT immunoassays:** antibody-associated light → calibrated ng/L or pg/mL | Platform-specific performance, serial change, and clinical context matter. Myocardial injury and myocardial infarction are distinct; troponin concentration alone does not establish an ischemic cause. [218,219,252] |
| Anticoagulant-associated effect in citrated plasma | **Chromogenic anti-Xa:** residual enzyme-dependent color → drug-matched activity or concentration | Heparin IU/mL and drug ng/mL are different results; calibration, antithrombin context, timing, and coexisting drugs determine attribution. [68,220] |
| Red-cell antigen/antibody pattern | **Column agglutination:** spatial retention → ordinal 0–4+ grade | Reagents determine typing, screening, or antiglobulin interpretation; ordinal grade is not concentration or genotype. [221] |
| Newborn metabolic screening in dried blood | **Tandem MS:** ion-area/internal-standard ratios → amino-acid/acylcarnitine quantities, µmol/L, and patterns | Abnormal screening requires biochemical or molecular confirmation; specimen representation and actual nutritional/developmental biology can change the pattern. [222,223] |
| HER2 expression in fixed tumor tissue | **IHC:** spatial chromogenic membrane staining → ordinal expression assessment | Protein expression differs from gene amplification measured by ISH/FISH; use the tumor-specific interpretation framework. [69] |

Immunoassays need calibration, low-level performance, and interference assessment. Biotin can interfere with susceptible immunoassays, with effects that depend on assay design. Anti-Xa results require a matching calibration and sampling context: when more than one drug with anti-Xa activity is present, the assay can reflect their combined effects. Correlation with LC-MS/MS does not demonstrate quantitative interchangeability. [68,219,220]

Column agglutination produces a graded pattern interpreted according to the specific reagents and testing purpose. Apply the controls and interpretation rules for that format. Newborn screening likewise depends on specimen quality and collection context; an abnormal screening pattern prompts the program’s designated confirmatory pathway rather than constituting a final diagnosis. [221,222,224]

HER2 interpretation requires suitable preservation, staining controls, tumor context, and the relevant scoring framework. Protein expression and gene amplification are related but different measurements. Evidence about specimen handling should match the biomarker: a single-specimen ER, PR, and Ki67 study cannot directly validate HER2 scoring. [69,225]

Clinical method choice rests on the intended decision, specimen, population, and validation evidence. A point-of-care format does not specify the measured property or guarantee equivalence with a central-laboratory method. Check the applicable clinical and regulatory requirements for that use.

## Product quality and process measurements

Identity, quantity, purity, potency, contamination, and process state are separate quality objectives. A physicochemical result can characterize a product attribute; a mechanism-linked response can support potency. Neither alone proves clinical effectiveness. Release decisions require product-specific specifications and evidence that the methods are suitable for their intended use. [70,71,81]

| Quality objective | Candidate family and signal-to-endpoint chain | Separate property still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Particle content distribution, including AAV characterization | **AUC, mass photometry, charge-detection MS, SEC-MALS with appropriate detectors:** hydrodynamic, mass, or detector responses → method-dependent distribution estimates | Genome identity/integrity and biological potency require separate evidence. Do not assume all methods resolve or classify the same particle populations. [8,226,227] |
| RNA 5′-cap structure | **Cap-fragment high-resolution MS:** separated species/ion areas → cap identity and relative fractions | RNA integrity, poly-A-related properties, delivery, lifetime, and translation. [229,230] |
| Endotoxin-associated content | **rFC/rCR/LAL:** calibrated enzyme-associated response → endotoxin units, such as EU/mL | Endotoxin testing does not measure every pyrogenic substance. Establish suitability for the product and testing purpose. [228,255,256] |
| Relative mechanism-linked function | Biological response plus **parallel-line/4PL/5PL analysis** → relative potency ratio or % | Other mechanisms and clinical effectiveness; analysis models are not themselves biological assay families. [70,231] |
| Cell-product signaling or killing | **Release, flow, impedance, NFAT-associated reporter** measurements → lysis, target state, kinetic index, or signaling response | Signaling does not guarantee killing; target context and antigen dependence remain necessary. [50,232] |
| Site-resolved product attributes | **LC-MS multi-attribute method, MAM:** peptide mass/fragmentation → relative modified/unmodified areas and new-feature flags | Unique clinical consequence, complete purity coverage, and calibrated impurity mass; total ion current is an instrument-signal denominator. [233] |
| Host DNA or specified microbial nucleic acid | **qPCR/dPCR:** calibrated amplification/occupancy → target amount or concentration with an explicit product/sample denominator | Target quantity alone does not establish fragment integrity, organism viability, or overall product safety. [14,81] |
| Protein size/charge heterogeneity | **CE-SDS, cIEF/icIEF:** migration/focusing peaks → relative species areas, pI, charge profile | Molecular origin of a peak/shift; orthogonal structural information is needed for identity. [71,234] |
| Synthesis-associated process state | **CFPS-associated reporter/product monitoring:** optical/activity signal → relative response or a calibrated product-specific quantity | Correct folding, purity, final potency, and productive manufacturing scale; CFPS is a process, not the monitoring assay. [235,236] |

Particle methods share an application while measuring different physical properties. Sedimentation depends on hydrodynamics, mass photometry on calibrated event contrast, and SEC-MALS on separation and detector models. Evaluate resolution and recovery using characterized populations relevant to the product. Empty/full labels are only as informative as the method’s ability to distinguish the underlying distribution. [8,226,227]

Endotoxin selection begins with the required claim. Recombinant factor C (rFC) and recombinant cascade reagents (rCR) are distinct non-animal-derived reagent systems for bacterial endotoxin testing. Matrix interference, sample handling, and product-specific suitability still need assessment. Endotoxin results do not establish absence of all pyrogens, and this guide supplies no generic product-release limit. [228,255,256]

Relative potency compares the test material’s biological response with a reference preparation. Parallel-line or parallel-curve approaches require an appropriate common response relationship; a fitted curve alone does not establish that relationship. Define system suitability, sample suitability, range, and precision before using a potency estimate for a decision. Choose the biological response first and justify its analysis model for the product. [70,231,258]

For cell-product potency, spontaneous/maximum target-release references define specific lysis in a release assay. They do not convert every impedance or reporter response into lysis. Impedance can reveal kinetics in adherent targets but combines adhesion, proliferation, and loss; suspension-target contexts may need another route. A scalable signaling surrogate needs evidence connecting it to the function required for the product. [38,50]

MAM reference assignments and preparation controls help distinguish product changes from analytical artifacts. Electrophoretic methods add size- or charge-related fingerprints, while molecular contamination tests address specified targets. The product-control strategy must identify which quality attributes each method covers and which remain unmeasured. [71,81,233,234]

## Plants, food, and ecological systems

An intact leaf, processed food, whole soil, environmental water, and a sampler extract have different denominators and interpretation. Select the property and matrix together. A plant fluorescence response is not nutritional protein; a soil respiratory profile is not a taxonomic census; a sequence found in water is not a count of local living organisms.

### Plant physiology and food properties

Pulse-amplitude-modulated (PAM) chlorophyll fluorescence and OJIP transient analysis supply photochemistry-associated endpoints. Gas exchange measures carbon assimilation and can provide complementary evidence. [72,237]

| Question | Supported route and endpoint | Main limitation and useful complement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Has photochemistry changed? | **PAM fluorescence:** Fv/Fm, Y(II), NPQ/qP; **OJIP transient analysis:** indices such as PIabs | Specify light history and measurement mode. These dimensionless ratios and indices describe photochemistry; carbon assimilation requires a gas-exchange measurement. [72,237,238] |
| What nitrogen-associated crude-protein estimate applies? | **Dumas/Kjeldahl:** nitrogen measurement → %N and conversion-dependent crude protein | Non-protein nitrogen and matrix-dependent factors prevent universal true/nutritional-protein inference; amino-acid profiling is an alternative requiring a separate comparison. [73,239] |
| Does processed food contain recognized prolamin material? | **Sandwich R5 immunoassay:** recognition-associated signal → calibrated content within its validated scope | Check suitability for the food and processing state. The result concerns recognized prolamin material; it does not measure all allergens or predict an individual clinical response. [240] |
| Is an origin/adulteration isotope pattern present? | **Isotope-ratio MS/SNIF-NMR:** calibrated bulk/positional isotope ratios | Reference dependence, purity, and signal quality constrain origin inference; dynamic tracer flux is a different measurement. [152] |
| Is a phytosanitary target sequence detected? | Targeted **qPCR**, including Xylella-associated detection: fluorescence → Cq and context-specific classification | Plant inhibition, sampling, and contamination controls qualify detection; DNA is not viability, severity, or transmission. [241,242] |

Chlorophyll fluorescence reflects the partitioning of excitation energy among photochemistry, heat dissipation, and fluorescence. Dark- and light-adapted endpoints answer different questions, so retain the illumination history and biological reference. Gas-exchange measurements provide complementary evidence about carbon assimilation. A fluorescence response alone does not establish reduced carbon fixation or permanent stress damage. [72,237,243]

Nitrogen recovery and protein inference are separate questions. Dumas and Kjeldahl quantify nitrogen under their respective method conditions; conversion to crude protein requires a justified factor. Non-protein nitrogen prevents a universal equivalence between nitrogen-derived crude protein and nutritionally available protein. R5 immunoassays address recognized prolamins as a measure of gluten within their validated scope, not total nutritional protein. [73,239,240]

Plant target amplification needs extraction, amplification-competence, and contamination controls appropriate to the matrix. Define positive, negative, indeterminate, and invalid results explicitly. Target DNA detection cannot establish seed vigor, crop performance, or plant–microbe function without measurements of those properties. [241]

### Soil, environmental DNA, and contaminant sampling

**MicroResp** measures selected substrate-associated respiration in whole soil. CO₂-dependent indicator absorbance is converted to a calibrated rate, for example µg CO₂-C g⁻¹ dry soil h⁻¹. Retain the dry-soil denominator, incubation conditions, and water-only background. Catabolic response patterns describe the tested substrate set, not taxonomic diversity or overall ecosystem resilience. [74,244]

**Environmental DNA/metabarcoding** detects marker sequences and assigns taxa. Field, extraction, and amplification blanks address different contamination sources; known communities help assess detection and classification. Report the marker, sampling design, reference database, and assignment model. Read fractions describe sequence representation. Interpreting them as local living-organism abundance requires additional evidence linking DNA recovery and detection to the population of interest. [245,246]

Targeted **LC-MS/MS residue/toxin quantification** requires analyte- and matrix-specific evidence for recovery, selectivity, calibration, and response. A method validated in one matrix is a candidate for transfer, not automatically a validated method in another. Chemical content, bioavailable exposure, and biological effect remain separate claims. [141,142]

**SPATT passive sampling** captures analytes during deployment. Report the measured sampler loading with its resin amount and deployment context. Resin-associated loading and water concentration have different denominators; converting between them requires an independently supported sampling model. Capture can indicate toxin occurrence without establishing its ambient concentration or causal role in an ecological effect. [75,247]

## Three worked selections

The settings and result patterns in these examples are **hypothetical**. Each applies the measurement distinctions above to build a shortlist for a stated claim.

### “Does this treatment make cells healthier?”

Suppose a researcher has a limited adherent-cell specimen, sees a lower metabolic screening signal after treatment, and wants to distinguish cell loss, slower cycling, and altered metabolism. “Health” does not select an assay. Here the useful construct is retained cellular integrity and proliferative capacity; the relevant properties include cell number, metabolic state, membrane leakage, and DNA synthesis during the biological interval. [34,35,156]

| Selection step | Defensible choice in the hypothetical case | Claim boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establish the population context | Shortlist image-based or cytometric counts, with a defined sampling denominator | A count requires an object definition and does not alone establish cellular function. |
| Interpret the screening endpoint | Retain the ATP- or tetrazolium-associated response as metabolic evidence | A lower proxy signal is not proof of death. |
| Add a different property | Shortlist LDH for membrane leakage, with spontaneous/background and maximum-release context | Leakage differs from metabolic suppression and does not uniquely identify death mechanism. |
| Test the division interpretation | Shortlist EdU/BrdU detection for DNA synthesis, considering label effects and interval | Less incorporation does not count every lost division or prove irreversible arrest. |
| Examine dynamics if needed | Consider impedance for response timing, then use the selected specific endpoints to interpret it | Cell Index combines coverage and adhesion with other responses. |
| Confirm a proposed death pathway | Add complementary death-associated markers if the biological claim requires them | Marker concordance must retain time and specificity rather than make one marker definitive. |

The choices follow different measured properties and physical artifacts. [35,38,158,159]

If the hypothetical follow-up showed lower ATP without a corresponding LDH increase, it would leave metabolic suppression, cytostasis, response timing, and interference open. Lower DNA synthesis would add evidence about cycling during the measured interval, while a death-marker pattern would address a different proposed mechanism. A single “viability IC50” would collapse those questions too early. [34,35,160]

Sample consumption sharpens the choice: LDH supernatant sampling may leave the cellular layer available, whereas lytic ATP acquisition uses it. A repeat of the same metabolic assay could improve precision while preserving its central biological ambiguity. The defensible output is a small battery whose endpoints retain their own meanings, not one composite health label without a measurement model. [34,159]

### “Are these immune cells effective against the target?”

Suppose a hypothetical target-recognition study has a limited PBMC specimen and needs to distinguish recognition, mediator response, and killing. The intended claim is target-associated effector capacity in the represented system, rather than overall immune protection. Those stages require related but separate evidence. [47,48,50]

Start with peptide–MHC multimer measurements if detectable receptor recognition is the missing property. The observable is reagent-associated fluorescence and the endpoint a positive fraction within a stated lineage. Specificity/scaffold evidence and avidity matter; recognition can coexist with functional unresponsiveness, while low-affinity responders can be undercounted. A higher positive fraction therefore does not alone establish more effective cells. [47]

For the response stage, choose ELISpot/FluoroSpot when secretion frequency or combined mediator footprints are central. Choose ICS when the response must be attached to a phenotyped parent population. The decision turns on what information is needed, not a universal sensitivity ranking. Report SFU per well or defined input for the former, and mediator-positive percentage within the explicit parent gate for the latter; do not compare their numbers as if they share a denominator or temporal property. [48,182]

If the claim specifically requires lytic competence, add a target-killing endpoint. CD107a-associated degranulation could be informative, but is not sufficient because release and successful lysis can separate. Background/spontaneous target loss, range, specificity, and effector context qualify that endpoint. In the hypothetical case, recognition plus cytokines plus degranulation without demonstrated target loss would support several response stages while leaving killing unestablished. No combination here automatically becomes a correlate of protection in a person. [49,50]

### “Is this crop experiencing damaging stress?”

Suppose a hypothetical crop study compares leaves after an environmental change and asks whether a response indicates impaired photosynthetic function rather than a transient optical or physiological shift. Define which meaning of stress matters: photochemical behavior, carbon-assimilation capacity, or subsequent growth. A fluorescence ratio addresses the first; the latter two need distinct evidence. [72,237]

PAM fluorescence or OJIP transient analysis is a reasonable first candidate for a photochemical question. Specify the light state, biological reference, and endpoint rather than constructing a generic “stress score.” The result remains a fluorescence-derived ratio or index, interpreted for the species and measurement conditions. [72,238,243]

A hypothetical shift in that endpoint would support changed photochemical behavior under the stated comparison. If the claim requires reduced carbon assimilation, gas exchange is a complementary family to consider; A–Ci and capacity endpoints require the gas-exchange measurement and its own interpretation model. Narrowing further would require units, specimen-quality constraints, model assumptions, controls, and comparative practical evidence. If the real decision concerns growth or crop performance, that outcome needs its own measurement: fluorescence cannot establish it by interpretation alone. [72,237]

The selection therefore depends on the intended claim: fluorescence for photochemical behavior, gas exchange for carbon assimilation, and a growth or performance endpoint for crop outcome. Agreement across these layers can support a broader interpretation; disagreement identifies the next question to resolve.

## When the evidence supports only a family

The comparisons above cover selected assay families, not every taxon, specimen, or application. Where a route is only outlined, the guide can structure the question but cannot select a particular implementation without additional evidence.

| Underdeveloped route | What can be decided now | Information needed before a named-method choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classical genomic/hybridization routes, including arrays, blots, conventional in situ, genotyping/copy-number, and nascent/run-on RNA | Decide whether identity, quantity, location, or synthesis is required; the modern sequencing examples do not span all these properties | Variant boundaries, specimen chain, observable/units, specificity, normalization, and comparative controls.  |
| Protein turnover, secretome, and specialized molecular-form routes | Decide whether a peptide, epitope, intact form, occupancy, or time-dependent protein quantity is the claim | Target-specific calibration, recovery, molecular linkage, denominator, and interference; a clinical immunoassay is not a complete generic profile.  |
| Broad cellular/innate/organ physiology and life-course functions | Define the required process or outcome and biological system | Construct-specific endpoints, competence and competing-explanation evidence, experimental unit, and translation context.  |
| Underprofiled imaging, chemical sensors, elemental/speciation, and multimodal routes | Decide whether identity, location, quantity, rate, or joint state is needed | Detector-specific references, effective spatial/temporal resolution, capture/assignment, and specimen comparability.  |
| Wider community, clinical, plant, food, and ecological measurements | Separate molecular potential, realized response, clinical state, or field exposure from mere detection | Organism/matrix-specific validation, intended-use populations or field models, denominators, and comparator context.  |
| Wider product/process methods and modalities | Separate identity/purity/function/contamination/process questions | Attribute coverage, mechanism linkage, reference lifecycle, stability/comparability, and product-specific suitability.  |
| Supporting sampling, authentication, preservation, extraction/fractionation, separation, and transfer processes | Identify where material or signal can be lost or altered | Representativeness, recovery, commutability, batch/version effects, and reproducibility; supporting processes do not automatically supply their own biological endpoint.  |

A practical selection record can now be completed in five steps:

1. State the biological claim and the alternative explanations it must distinguish.
2. Specify each required property, specimen/system, interval, and observational unit.
3. Write the observable-to-endpoint chain, including units, denominator, calibration, and model assumptions.
4. Compare supported candidates for artifacts, specimen demand/reuse, resolution, equipment, analysis, throughput, and intended-use validation; keep unavailable fields explicit.
5. Select controls for named failure modes and complementary measurements for the biological links the first endpoint cannot establish.

These steps keep measurement, confirmation, and interpretation connected.

The stopping point is a claim that the proposed measurements can actually support. Sometimes one assay is enough; sometimes the broad question separates into several properties and a small complementary battery. Sometimes the sources justify only a family and the evidence needed to narrow it. In each case, the defensible choice preserves the distance between acquired signal, measured property, and biological conclusion.

## Source and scope note

This is a selective assay-selection reference. It connects measured properties to defensible claims and outlines how to compare candidates. It is not an exhaustive catalog, a clinical decision algorithm, or a collection of laboratory protocols. The worked selections are hypothetical.

The bibliography includes research papers, reviews, reporting guidelines, standards, and technical documentation. Manufacturer documentation describes particular implementations; it does not establish independent superiority. Method names, numerical performance, and clinical or product decisions must be interpreted for the stated specimen and intended use. Some routes remain less fully developed, as summarized above. The original bibliography and numbering are retained for traceability; additional sources support the clarified passages, and entries no longer cited directly remain as background reading.
