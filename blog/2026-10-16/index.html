What do you want to measure?

An assay is useful when its result answers the question that prompted the measurement. “Is it healthy?”, “Does it work?”, and “Has it changed?” are starting points. Each becomes a different shortlist once the property, specimen, interval, and competing explanations are specified.

This reference helps researchers move from that question to a justified assay or family of assays. It spans molecular, cellular, tissue, organismal, clinical, product-quality, plant, and environmental measurements. Coverage is selective: a family-level recommendation identifies the next decision, rather than promising a complete catalog or a validated method for every specimen.

Use the guide in three passes: find your question below; compare the linked methods; complete the selection worksheet. For a known method, use the assay and acronym index. The worked selections show how several measurements can answer different parts of one question.

Turn the construct into a measurement claim

“Health,” “stress,” “viability,” and “function” describe constructs: biological ideas whose meanings depend on the question. Cell health might mean maintaining membrane integrity, sustaining energy metabolism, continuing DNA synthesis, or retaining a specialized function. Plant stress might mean a photochemical change, lower carbon assimilation, or reduced growth. A proxy useful for one meaning may leave another entirely unmeasured.[34,35,72]

The selection sequence is research question → biological construct → measurable property and specimen → observable and endpoint → assay family → candidate comparison and confirmation. Its practical product is a claim sentence: In this specimen or system, over this biological interval, I need to determine this property, expressed by this endpoint, well enough to distinguish these alternatives. The sequence is a reasoning framework; it does not replace validation for a particular intended use.

An analyte is the component being examined; a measurand is the quantity intended to be measured, specified with its system and conditions. “Protein X” names an analyte but leaves open concentration, catalytic activity, binding, modification, and localization. The observable is acquired signal: photons, ion current, voltage, images, or recorded events. The endpoint is the reported result after classification, calibration, normalization, or fitting. A fluorescence trace, a relative fold change, and a disease interpretation occupy different stages of the chain.[76]

These distinctions recur across the molecular, cellular, spatial, immune, and product-quality evidence. “Direct for a property” still means conditional on the measurement model: calibrated ATP signal can establish something about ATP while remaining a proxy for surviving cells. A counted transcript is a detected and assigned molecule, not proof that every source molecule was recovered.[34,70,77]

Keep four classification dimensions separate. Principle is how a property generates signal; biological scale is molecular, cellular, tissue, or organismal context; format is bulk, single-cell, spatial, multiplexed, or longitudinal organization; application is the intended research, clinical, environmental, or quality decision. Mass spectrometry can serve all those applications. A three-dimensional culture is a model, and high-throughput screening is an arrangement of measurements. Neither specifies one measurand.[78,79]

A changed format can improve access to heterogeneity or timing without changing the underlying property. It can also alter sampling, artifacts, calibration, or even the biological system. Droplet and chamber dPCR share occupancy logic but require appropriate volume information; single-cell proteomics is not simply bulk proteomics with guaranteed identical precision; moving from a secretion spot to intracellular staining changes what and when the immune response is observed. Demonstrate comparability at the endpoint level rather than infer it from a family name.[14,48,80]

A meaningful practical comparison uses the same specimen, endpoint, required performance, and accounting boundary. Acquisition time alone is not turnaround time; a quoted sample volume may exclude repeats and confirmation. The evidence assembled here does not support a universal cost, consumption, or throughput ranking across families.

Controls should answer named doubts. Blanks assess background; positive responses assess system competence; specificity controls examine recognition; calibration references support quantity; recovery and matrix controls examine loss or altered response; independent biological endpoints test competing interpretations. Repeating the same optical proxy improves repeatability but may preserve the same interference. Orthogonal confirmation changes the physical principle or examines a different necessary property.[34,81]

The limit of blank, limit of detection, and limit of quantitation respectively concern blank response, reliable distinction from background, and acceptable quantitative performance. A detectable result may remain below the quantitative range. A valid negative, analytical failure, missing observation, and censored value are distinct. Precision describes spread; bias describes systematic difference from a relevant reference. High correlation between methods does not establish agreement or interchangeability.[76,82,83]

Calibration itself needs context. Traceability links a result through a calibration chain with uncertainty at each step; a reference material's commutability concerns whether it behaves appropriately across methods for the intended specimen comparison. Type A uncertainty comes from repeated observations and Type B from other information, rather than from a biological/instrumental split. Many cells, fields, sequence reads, or repeated times within one specimen do not automatically provide independent biological replicates.[76,84,85,86]

Represent the final selection in a record that another reader can interpret: specimen, measured property, principle, method/variant, observable, analysis model, endpoint/unit, controls, calibration, and intended claim. OBI and BAO describe processes or screening concepts; LOINC structures clinical observations; ISA connects investigation, specimens, and assays; EDAM describes computational operations. Reporting frameworks help preserve provenance. Their terms should be mapped to the actual measured property and analysis model; a checklist or ontology identifier does not validate a measurement.[78,79,87,88,89]

The comparisons address measurement, selection, and interpretation. Method names identify families or implementations, not universally transferable specifications. Numerical limits, clinical interpretation, and product decisions require evidence for the particular specimen, platform version, and intended use. The source and scope note describes coverage and interpretation boundaries.

Binding, catalysis, and molecular structure

Binding strength, binding speed, catalytic inhibition, assembly, and stability are related questions with different answers. The equilibrium dissociation constant, KD, has concentration units; association and dissociation rates describe approach to and escape from a complex. An IC50 is the concentration producing half-inhibition in the particular assay, whereas a Ki requires an appropriate inhibition model. Avidity includes multivalent retention and rebinding, so a slowly dissociating multivalent interaction is not necessarily strong single-site affinity.[1,7]

Choose interaction measurements by the parameter needed

SPR and BLI are optical interaction measurements; ITC measures heat. STD means saturation-transfer-difference NMR, while WaterLOGSY is a water–ligand magnetization-transfer method. Their common binding purpose does not make their acquired signals equivalent.[1,2,4,90]

Separate catalytic rate from a binding signal

For a catalytic question, first decide whether the required endpoint is an overall reaction rate or an individual mechanistic step. A steady-state activity assay measures substrate consumption or product formation over an interpretable interval. A calibrated rate can be reported as amount or concentration per time; normalization to enzyme quantity adds an explicit denominator. An endpoint amount divided by elapsed time is not automatically an initial rate, and an apparent Michaelis constant is not generally a binding dissociation constant.[6]

These choices separate the biological reaction from its detection chain. Direct measurement still has analytical artifacts; a coupled signal needs evidence that its change can be attributed to the target reaction.[6,81]

Stopped-flow records early optical reaction traces; quench-flow examines products associated with a defined reaction age. Fitted microscopic rates can help investigate steps hidden by a steady-state aggregate, but instrument dead time can hide the earliest behavior and different mechanisms can fit similar decays. Baselines, mixing artifacts, and the proposed reaction model determine how much mechanistic discrimination the trace earns. These candidates need rapid-reaction instrumentation plus optical or product-analysis capacity.[92]

Tight-binding inhibition needs a mass-balance treatment when enzyme binding materially depletes free inhibitor. The Morrison model uses measured velocities and active-enzyme information to estimate an apparent inhibition constant in concentration units. Unknown active fraction and incomplete equilibration can confuse tight and slow binding. Ordinary IC50-to-Ki conversion is justified only under its competition and concentration assumptions. For slow-binding inhibition, activation, or biochemical reconstitution, the rate regime, active fraction, and reporter chain must support the proposed model; a familiar equation cannot supply missing mechanistic evidence.[7]

Fluorescence energy-transfer methods, including FRET/TR-FRET, and AlphaScreen supply interaction-associated signals in screening formats. AlphaScreen's proximity-dependent singlet-oxygen luminescence is vulnerable to quenching and chemical interference. Detection-chemistry counter-screens can test whether a compound changes the reporter instead of the biology. Screen-control separation, including a Z-prime statistic, assesses assay robustness rather than target specificity; a structural interference alert is a prediction, not demonstrated interference.[93,94,95]

DNA, RNA, and regulatory state

Ask whether you need a specified sequence, its abundance, linkage along a molecule, a cellular distribution, a base modification, or chromatin context. Targeted amplification can answer a narrow quantity question without describing the transcriptome; an accessibility map does not itself establish gene expression. For RNA, steady-state abundance combines synthesis and degradation and does not establish protein translation.[20,21,22,102]

Targeted presence and quantity

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) tracks amplification; RT-qPCR adds reverse transcription for RNA. Digital PCR (dPCR) uses partition occupancy. Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) offer constant-temperature detection routes.[13,14,15,16]

Conventional relative RT-qPCR analysis assumes an appropriate target/reference efficiency relationship. Efficiency-aware analysis, including the Pfaffl approach, does not solve unstable references, RNA degradation, poor extraction, or unequal reverse transcription. A real-time thermocycler and target-specific validation are required; selecting a faster or simpler detection chemistry cannot replace them. MIQE 2.0 provides an updated reporting framework for judging whether these decisions are adequately documented.[13,104,108]

dPCR removes conventional standard-curve dependence, not measurement uncertainty. Positive partitions can contain multiple molecules: the occupancy estimate is λ = −ln(1−p), where p is the positive fraction. A concentration requires a valid volume denominator. Intermediate fluorescence, variable partition size, coalescence, and nearly complete positivity compromise inference. A rare-variant detection claim must retain its sample input, background, decision rule, and uncertainty; figures from unmatched conditions cannot rank dPCR against qPCR universally. Reporting with dMIQE preserves the necessary context without proving the model correct.[14,105]

Isothermal amplification is a candidate for targeted detection where its simpler acquisition fits the intended setting. It still requires matrix-specific competence and specificity evidence. Time to positivity is a signal-derived endpoint, not automatically an absolute target quantity. A positive target sequence establishes molecular detection; disease, viable burden, and infectivity remain separate questions.[15,16,54]

HiFi and nanopore are separated by their physical signals and the contiguity/accuracy requirements they can address. Read-length and accuracy comparisons are implementation- and benchmark-specific. Protein amount, form, activity, and neighborhood

“Protein level” can mean digested-peptide signal, accessible epitopes, intact molecular forms, or a modified fraction. A proteoform is a particular form of a gene product, including sequence and post-translational differences; a peptidoform is a particular peptide form. Digestion removes linkage between distant features, so an accurately identified modified peptide does not establish the complete proteoform from which it came.[24,116]

Fluids, antibodies, and pathway function

Soluble-mediator multiplex assays measure fluid-level cytokine/chemokine profiles rather than the secreting cell. Bead arrays such as Luminex associate reporter fluorescence with bead identity; electrochemiluminescence such as MSD uses electrically generated light; PEA converts dual recognition into a nucleic-acid signal. Standards can yield concentration, such as pg/mL, when validation supports that conversion. NPX remains relative. Anti-drug antibody, ADA, assays assess binding reactivity toward a therapeutic product. Unverified detail: SPEAD/ACE are interference-management variants; their suitability depends on how the treatment affects both interference and the intended antibody signal.[51,186,187]

Evidence limitation: Supporting content for the named variants and their interference-versus-signal trade-off has not been verified. General ADA guidance does not confirm each variant.

Susceptibility and biofilms

The biofilm endpoint MBEC means minimum biofilm eradication concentration in the represented recovery model; XTT is a tetrazolium-reduction readout, while crystal violet measures retained biomass.[58,194]

The minimum inhibitory concentration, MIC, measures planktonic growth inhibition in concentration units, commonly µg/mL or mg/L. “Susceptible” or “resistant” adds a drug–organism–interpretive-framework judgment; it is not part of the raw growth endpoint. Clinical categorization requires the applicable organism, agent, method, and interpretive-framework version. Resistance-marker detection asks a different molecular question and is not a substitute for observed phenotype.[57,58]

For a biofilm, distinguish recoverable survival, retained biomass, and metabolic reduction. MBEC concerns a concentration associated with no detected surviving/recoverable growth in a model. Crystal-violet absorbance includes matrix and live/dead material. XTT reduction is a metabolic proxy. Reduced XTT with retained biomass can therefore be a coherent result, while reduced staining does not establish eradication. Recovery and background/interference evidence serve different purposes, and cross-laboratory MBEC comparability remains unresolved. MIC and MBEC should not be converted using a general factor.[58,194,195]

Soil, environmental DNA, and contaminant sampling

MicroResp examines selected substrate-associated respiration in whole soil. CO₂-induced indicator absorbance becomes a calibrated rate, expressed as µg CO₂-C g⁻¹ dry soil h⁻¹. Water-only background helps separate basal response; Unverified details: abiotic carbonate CO₂, moisture, gas diffusion, and normalization qualify the result. Support for this specific set of limitations has not been verified; the units describe the reported endpoint, not a universal correction. Retaining soil interactions differs from extracted community-response systems such as Biolog EcoPlate. Catabolic evenness describes the selected response set, not species diversity or overall ecosystem resilience.[74,244]

Environmental DNA/metabarcoding recovers marker sequences and assigns taxa. Aquatic COI coverage is more developed here than terrestrial botanical rbcL/matK/ITS; wider 16S/18S/ITS contexts also occur. Field, extraction, and amplification blanks address separate contamination sources, and known communities examine uneven recovery/detection. Unverified detail: Shedding, degradation, transport, marker mismatch, database gaps, and inhibition separate a detected sequence from a local living organism. ASVs/OTUs and read fractions remain processed sequence categories rather than individual counts; occupancy probability adds a sampling/detection model, not certainty.[245,246]

Evidence limitation: The eDNA literature supports detection limitations in its studied settings. Support for every listed process and for the ASV/OTU-versus-individual-count interpretation has not been verified individually; read fractions are not used here as validated organism counts.

Targeted LC-MS/MS residue/toxin quantification requires matrix-specific recovery and response evidence. Unverified detail: A method in watery produce cannot be presumed equivalent in dried herbs, where concentrated co-extracts alter ionization. Matrix-matched calibration and suitable isotope standards address response, but each matrix/analyte still needs evidence. Chemical content, bioavailable exposure, and biological effect are separate claims.[141,142]

Evidence limitation: Support for this specific produce-versus-herb matrix example has not been verified. It is an explicitly unverified example, not a demonstrated comparison or correction factor.

SPATT passive sampling integrates analyte capture over environmental exposure. Unverified detail: Extract analysis reports resin-associated loading, such as ng/g resin; it is not water concentration in µg/L without a kinetic conversion. Food microbial quality/spoilage and functional properties, heavy-metal content/speciation/bioavailability, biodegradation, ecological process rates, exposure indicators, broader environmental toxicity, and remote-sensing comparisons remain incomplete selection coverage. Source-supported microbial, chemical, or embryo routes can supply particular measurements, but cannot by themselves complete those larger constructs or prove field relevance.

Three worked selections

The settings and result patterns in these examples are hypothetical. Each applies the measurement distinctions above to build a shortlist for a stated claim.

“Does this treatment make cells healthier?”

Suppose a researcher has a limited adherent-cell specimen, sees a lower metabolic screening signal after treatment, and wants to distinguish cell loss, slower cycling, and altered metabolism. “Health” does not select an assay. Here the useful construct is retained cellular integrity and proliferative capacity; the relevant properties include cell number, metabolic state, membrane leakage, and DNA synthesis during the biological interval.[34,35,156]

If the hypothetical follow-up showed lower ATP without a corresponding LDH increase, it would leave metabolic suppression, cytostasis, response timing, and interference open. Sample consumption sharpens the choice: LDH supernatant sampling may leave the cellular layer available, whereas lytic ATP acquisition uses it. A repeat of the same metabolic assay could improve precision while preserving its central biological ambiguity. The defensible output is a small battery whose endpoints retain their own meanings, not one composite health label without a measurement model.[34,159]

“Are these immune cells effective against the target?”

Suppose a hypothetical target-recognition study has a limited PBMC specimen and needs to distinguish recognition, mediator response, and killing. The intended claim is target-associated effector capacity in the represented system, rather than overall immune protection. Those stages require related but separate evidence.[47,48,50]

Start with peptide–MHC multimer measurements if detectable receptor recognition is the missing property. The observable is reagent-associated fluorescence and the endpoint a positive fraction within a stated lineage. Specificity/scaffold evidence and avidity matter; recognition can coexist with functional unresponsiveness, while low-affinity responders can be undercounted. A higher positive fraction therefore does not alone establish more effective cells.[47]

For the response stage, choose ELISpot/FluoroSpot when secretion frequency or combined mediator footprints are central. Choose ICS when the response must be attached to a phenotyped parent population. The decision turns on what information is needed, not a universal sensitivity ranking. Report SFU per well or defined input for the former, and mediator-positive percentage within the explicit parent gate for the latter; do not compare their numbers as if they share a denominator or temporal property.[48,182]

If the claim specifically requires lytic competence, add a target-killing endpoint. CD107a-associated degranulation could be informative, but is not sufficient because release and successful lysis can separate. Background/spontaneous target loss, range, specificity, and effector context qualify that endpoint. In the hypothetical case, recognition plus cytokines plus degranulation without demonstrated target loss would support several response stages while leaving killing unestablished. No combination here automatically becomes a correlate of protection in a person.[49,50]

“Is this crop experiencing damaging stress?”

Suppose a hypothetical crop study compares leaves after an environmental change and asks whether a response indicates impaired photosynthetic function rather than a transient optical or physiological shift. Define which meaning of stress matters: photochemical behavior, carbon-assimilation capacity, or subsequent growth. A fluorescence ratio addresses the first; the latter two need distinct evidence.[72,237]

PAM/OJIP is a first shortlist for photochemical response. Specify dark/light state, conspecific reference context, and the desired endpoint—Fv/Fm, effective yield, quenching, or an OJIP-derived index—rather than attach a generic “stress score.” Unverified detail: Leaf optics, light history, and species can alter interpretation; cyanobacterial respiratory baselines especially prevent unchanged transfer of a plant reference. The observable is fluorescence, and the endpoint remains a photochemistry-associated ratio or index.[72,238,243]

Evidence limitation: Support for the specific cyanobacterial-baseline transfer limitation has not been verified from the checked passages. No plant or cyanobacterial diagnostic threshold or universal stress ranking is established.

A hypothetical shift in that endpoint would support changed photochemical behavior under the stated comparison. If the claim requires reduced carbon assimilation, gas exchange is a complementary family to consider; A–Ci and capacity endpoints require the gas-exchange measurement and its own interpretation model. Narrowing further would require units, specimen-quality constraints, model assumptions, controls, and comparative practical evidence. If the real decision concerns growth or crop performance, that outcome needs its own measurement: fluorescence cannot establish it by interpretation alone.[72,237]

This example ends with a justified first candidate and an explicit evidence requirement for the next measurement. That is a useful selection outcome when the available evidence does not yet justify a fully specified battery. It is more defensible than applying a species-independent fluorescence threshold to declare permanent damage.

A practical selection record can now be completed in five steps:

  1. State the biological claim and the alternative explanations it must distinguish.
  2. Specify each required property, specimen/system, interval, and observational unit.
  3. Write the observable-to-endpoint chain, including units, denominator, calibration, and model assumptions.
  4. Compare supported candidates for artifacts, specimen demand/reuse, resolution, equipment, analysis, throughput, and intended-use validation; keep unavailable fields explicit.
  5. Select controls for named failure modes and complementary measurements for the biological links the first endpoint cannot establish.

These steps keep measurement, confirmation, and interpretation connected.

The stopping point is a claim that the proposed measurements can actually support. Sometimes one assay is enough; sometimes the broad question separates into several properties and a small complementary battery. Sometimes the sources justify only a family and the evidence needed to narrow it. In each case, the defensible choice preserves the distance between acquired signal, measured property, and biological conclusion.

Source and scope note

This is a selective assay-selection reference, not an exhaustive ontology, a clinical decision algorithm, or a collection of executable biological protocols. Its central distinctions concern what a measurement observes, which claim it can support, and how to compare alternatives. Sensitive biological topics remain at the level of measurement principles and interpretation.

The references include original research, reviews, reporting guidelines, standards, and technical documentation. Manufacturer documentation supports descriptions of a method or product; it does not establish independent superiority. Coverage and source support are uneven, particularly for specialized controls, emerging methods, and cross-platform comparisons. A named family is not a validated method for every specimen or intended use. Unverified detail and Evidence limitation notes identify retained information whose specific published-source support has not been established; those details are not validated thresholds, selection rules, or grounds for ranking assays. A citation beside other content does not confirm a locally qualified claim. Scientific qualifications and the stated coverage gaps remain part of each selection.
